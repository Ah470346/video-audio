# Premise Originality and Audio Viability

Use this reference during open-ended ideation, premise selection, or diagnosis of a premise that feels interchangeable. It does not replace current-demand research, genre rules, or the user's approved premise.

## Ownership

`audio-story-engagement` owns concrete ideation. `audio_story_trend_researcher` may supply broad opportunity territories only. `audio_story_architect` may structure a selected premise but may not silently replace it.

## Premise Test

A working premise should identify:

```text
familiar pressure or pain
+ a character with a specific want
+ a mechanism that changes available choices
+ motivated resistance
+ a hard choice
+ a concrete cost
```

A topic is not a premise. A trope label is not a premise. A twist without a meaningful choice is not a premise.

## Difference Test

Compare candidate directions across:
- central point of view;
- arena and power structure;
- source of pressure;
- secret or evidence type;
- mechanism of conflict;
- irreversible choice;
- promised emotional result;
- ending state.

If several candidates differ only in names, jobs, or surface setting, merge or redesign them. Do not force novelty by making the mechanism difficult to understand in audio.

## Character Pressure

Before selecting a premise, answer:
- Why can the protagonist not simply leave, tell the truth, call authority, or refuse?
- What does the opposing person gain, fear, or protect?
- Which boundary do they knowingly cross?
- What choice changes the situation rather than merely revealing information?
- What does the protagonist risk that is specific to their values and circumstances?

`The character is foolish` is not an answer. Competence and blindness may coexist when the blindness is tied to a specific attachment, duty, status, fear, or false belief.

## Audio Viability

Prefer a premise that can be carried through:
- understandable actions and consequences;
- distinct relationships and forms of address;
- evidence, testimony, sound, space, or concrete decisions;
- a manageable cast and memory load;
- a central question that can be heard, progressed, and paid.

Redesign when the listener would need a diagram, visual identity matching, dense on-screen text, or repeated backtracking to understand the mechanism.

## Expansion Test

The premise must support more than one striking scene. Check whether it can produce:
- an old state that cannot continue;
- several causally different pressures or attempts;
- changing knowledge and relationships;
- at least one local payoff before the peak;
- a peak created by established choices;
- an ending state that pays the main genre promise.

Do not extend runtime through repeated accusation, interruption, suffering, or near-identical setbacks.

## Originality Boundaries

- Extract abstract conflict patterns, not event sequences, names, distinctive objects, wording, twists, or endings from sources.
- Change meaningful axes, not only names and locations.
- Do not treat a current trend as proof that one exact plot should be copied.
- Do not use skill examples as a generation bank.
- Familiar structure is allowed when character choice, mechanism, relationship, and consequence are specific.

## Selection Gate

Prefer the direction that has:
- a concrete listener question;
- motivated agency on more than one side;
- escalating cost without arbitrary coincidence;
- a reward appropriate to the main genre;
- enough distinct scene functions;
- a clear one-voice audio path;
- no safety or ethical dependency on exploiting an identifiable real case.

A simpler premise with human pressure and a strong choice beats a complicated premise whose novelty exists only in explanation.
