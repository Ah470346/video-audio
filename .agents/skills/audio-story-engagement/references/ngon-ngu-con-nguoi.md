# Human Semantic Fit And Natural Dialogue

Use this gate whenever prose is concrete and grammatically clean but still feels mechanical, overly clever, translated, or written to become a quote. It is mandatory when a profession, object system, or recurring image field enters dialogue about people or relationships.

## 1. Concrete Is Not Automatically Human

Replacing an abstraction with an object does not guarantee natural prose. Check both:

1. **Collocation:** would Vietnamese speakers normally combine these words in this meaning?
2. **Category fit:** does the verb treat a person, relationship, memory, or moral choice as though it literally obeyed the rules of an object, machine, account, case file, or interface?

Marked wording can be intentional. It fails when the only reason for it is that the character works with the source domain or the author wants a neat motif.

Examples:

- `cuộc hôn nhân tan vỡ`, `quan hệ rạn nứt`, `hai người bế tắc`, `không còn muốn sống cùng nhau` are ordinary human formulations with different meanings;
- `cuộc hôn nhân hỏng ở đâu`, `sửa cuộc hôn nhân cho đúng`, `tình cảm vận hành sai quy trình` are marked analogies. Keep one only when this exact character would naturally frame the issue that way and the scene earns the dehumanizing or defensive effect.

Check for translated English mappings such as `broken/fix a marriage` that remain understandable but produce unnatural Vietnamese collocation. Preserve meaning, not the source-language metaphor.

Do not apply a blanket word ban. `Hỏng` is natural for a plan, machine, meal, appointment, or mood in some contexts. Diagnose the target and meaning, not the token alone.

## 2. Profession Is Access, Not Destiny

A carpenter may notice weight, grain, joints, or a loose hinge. An accountant may notice omissions, debt, or imbalance. This gives the POV access to precise observations; it does not require every feeling to become furniture or every relationship to become a ledger.

Apply three ownership checks:

- **POV ownership:** did this image arise from what the focal character actually notices now?
- **Register ownership:** would the character think it privately, narrate it retrospectively, or say it aloud? These are different thresholds.
- **Speaker ownership:** has another speaker suddenly inherited the same professional metaphor only so they can set up or rebut a clever line?

One character's occupation must not colonize the narrator, all dialogue, and the other characters. Professional reflex is often more believable as an action, attention pattern, or practical decision than as an analogy.

## 3. Dialogue Is Action, Not Quote Production

Do not optimize direct speech for memorability, virality, symmetry, or a mic-drop ending. Resonance may result from accumulated pressure, but it is not an independent requirement.

Before keeping a polished line, run four tests:

1. **Plain-paraphrase test:** what does the speaker need the other person to understand or do right now? Write that in ordinary Vietnamese first.
2. **Mouth test:** would this person say these exact words, at this speed, under this pressure, to this listener?
3. **Reply test:** does the next speaker answer the actual human issue, or merely complete the author's metaphor?
4. **Residue test:** does the exchange leave hesitation, misunderstanding, practical consequence, or changed behavior, or does it end as a self-contained slogan?

Prefer the plain line when it performs the same action more credibly. Dialogue may be incomplete, blunt, evasive, repetitive in a character-specific way, or slightly awkward. Do not preserve filler, but do preserve human thought under pressure.

## 4. Aphorism Tennis And Metaphor Contagion

Flag an exchange when:

- one speaker introduces a polished analogy;
- the other instantly extends, reverses, or defeats it in the same semantic field;
- both lines could circulate alone as captions;
- neither line sounds like the characters' established everyday speech;
- the exchange states the theme more neatly than the characters could understand it at that point.

This is **aphorism tennis**. It often masquerades as subtext, chemistry, or intelligent banter. Real subtext hides or redirects a need; it does not require two characters to co-author one perfect metaphor.

**Forced occupational metaphor:**

> “Tám năm của chúng ta hỏng ở đâu?”
>
> “Anh sửa được đồ vật, sao không sửa được hôn nhân?”
>
> “Đồ vật không giả vờ rằng nó vẫn ổn.”

The words are clear, but the relationship has been reduced to a repair problem, and the second speaker borrows the first speaker's object vocabulary solely to cue a comeback.

**Human-first direction:**

> “Từ lúc nào chúng ta thôi nói thật với nhau?”
>
> Anh im một lúc. “Chắc từ lúc em cứ nói không sao để mình khỏi cãi tiếp.”

The repair is not to find a prettier object metaphor. Name the interpersonal behavior, let the reply take time when needed, and allow the two speakers to use different language.

## 5. Mandatory Semantic-Naturalness Audit

For every conspicuous metaphor and every high-impact dialogue cluster:

```text
Literal target:
Source domain:
Exact mapping:
Ordinary Vietnamese alternative:
Why this character owns the marked wording:
Why it belongs in speech rather than narration/thought:
Does the next speaker unnaturally inherit the same field?
What human behavior or consequence remains after the image is removed?
```

Reject or rewrite when the mapping is only `character has job X -> character describes love/family/life as X`, when the ordinary alternative is more believable without losing story action, or when the image exists mainly to manufacture a quotable exchange.

## 6. Review Labels

Use precise labels instead of `văn AI` alone:

- **category mismatch:** object/system verb makes human experience sound literally mechanical;
- **forced occupational metaphor:** profession supplies an analogy without lived thought or scene need;
- **metaphor contagion:** several speakers or the narrator inherit one field without ownership;
- **aphorism tennis:** consecutive turns build and rebut a polished maxim;
- **authorial ventriloquism:** character says the author's theme instead of pursuing an immediate goal;
- **over-finished speech:** syntax and insight are too complete for the character's pressure and established voice.

Treat these as semantic and character problems, not synonym problems. Repair the underlying thought, speaker ownership, and conversational action.
