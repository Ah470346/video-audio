# Professional Vietnamese Story Prose

This is the shared expert-quality gate for Vietnamese audio fiction. Use it during planning, drafting, final polish, and manual review. It prevents technically correct story systems from producing over-designed, imitative, psychologically omniscient, or recognizably AI-shaped prose.

## 1. Quality Hierarchy

When craft instructions compete, choose in this order:

1. truth of what happened;
2. truth of what each character can know, want, misunderstand, and do;
3. natural Vietnamese meaning and relationship register;
4. genre promise and causal movement;
5. audio clarity;
6. stylistic devices, motifs, callbacks, hooks, quotable lines, and other optional techniques.

Never sacrifice the first five to display the sixth. A plain sentence that belongs to the character is more professional than a clever sentence that belongs to the author.

Do not equate professional writing with maximal technique density. Expertise is selection: knowing which potentially good device not to use.

Enforce craft constraints silently. Do not let narration paraphrase the instruction it just obeyed—for example, explaining that a profession does not make the narrator understand everything, that a concrete detail is not proof, or that this is only an inference—unless the character has an independent in-story reason to think in those terms. Remove **guardrail leakage**: the story should embody the limit, not announce the writing rule.

## 2. Human Knowledge And Earned Insight

Separate four levels:

- **observation:** what was visibly or audibly done;
- **interpretation:** what the observer currently thinks it means;
- **pattern:** what repeated evidence supports;
- **truth:** what later action, history, or admission confirms.

A character may notice quickly. They may not diagnose another person's deepest habit, wound, marriage history, or motive as fact from one sharp exchange. Attraction, chemistry, professional skill, or narrator confidence does not grant psychological omniscience.

Run the **earned-insight test**:

```text
What exact behavior was observed?
What other explanations remain possible?
How certain is the observer allowed to be now?
What repeated evidence or disclosure confirms the pattern later?
Does the narration mark inference as inference?
```

Use `tôi đoán`, `tôi tưởng`, `có lẽ`, a provisional action, or later correction when certainty is not earned. A narrator may feel exposed by a stranger's remark; that feeling does not prove the stranger understood their hidden self correctly.

Expert characters still have domain limits. A therapist, investigator, doctor, lawyer, craftsperson, or manager is not automatically correct about people outside verified evidence.

## 3. Dialogue As Imperfect Contact

Dialogue is not a chess match in which every line must be a move. Judge the **exchange** by whether pressure, access, understanding, or relationship changes. Individual lines may:

- fail to answer;
- arrive late;
- repeat because the speaker cannot yet say the real thing;
- misunderstand part of the question;
- be practical or ordinary;
- lose force halfway through;
- expose an intention accidentally;
- leave the listener unsure.

Keep these only when they belong to character and pressure; do not preserve transcript filler.

Before a high-impact line, ask:

```text
What does this person want now?
What are they actually able to formulate now?
What would they avoid because of shame, rank, habit, or incomplete knowledge?
Would they say this, or is the author saying it through them?
Does the reply answer the person or merely complete a device?
```

Do not force subtext. Sometimes the truthful, direct, slightly clumsy sentence is the dramatic event. Do not make everyone witty, therapeutically articulate, or ready with the exact counterargument.

## 4. Scene Movement Without Visible Machinery

Use causal design internally, but hide the diagram. Not every short scene needs a visible `goal -> obstacle -> turn`, and not every audio minute needs a hook or reversal.

A scene or compact passage may earn its place by doing one or more of these:

- changing a decision, fact, relationship, risk, or access;
- letting a consequence become real;
- accumulating pressure or recognition;
- establishing normal life that a later event will violate;
- allowing an emotionally credible delay;
- giving comic or atmospheric experience promised by the genre;
- showing a failed attempt whose meaning becomes clear later.

The requirement is value and relation to the whole, not a visible beat formula. Quietness is not filler; technique labels showing through the prose are not pace.

Vary transition logic. Characters do not always process an event in the same scene. A residue may surface during an unrelated task later. Do not close every scene with a realization, slogan, reveal, decision, or motif.

### Closure And Hindsight Discipline

How paragraphs and scenes end is where template prose shows first, even when every individual sentence is good. Across a chapter, endings must vary: an action, a plain fact, a spoken line, an unanswered question, a half-thought. If most units close on an aphorism, a balanced antithesis, a thesis naming what the scene just showed, or a punchy fragment triple, unpolish most of them — uniformly clever closure is the most audible machine signature.

Three related shells to distrust:

- **Hindsight interjections and allusive posturing:** `tôi đâu biết...`, `mãi về sau tôi mới hiểu...`, `đó là sai lầm đầu tiên...`. A retrospective narrator may use one where the voice truly needs it; repeated, they pre-chew every beat and drain surprise. The deeper defect is allusive posturing: foreshadowing through an abstract riddle (`...đúng cái thứ mình sắp thua`) instead of the concrete stake in the narrator's own texture (`...một tháng trà sữa của mình đã bắt đầu đếm ngược`). Foreshadow with the named stake, or with a concrete action that shows the state (`đã bắt đầu nghĩ xem nên gọi vị trà sữa nào trước`); rewrite toward concreteness first, and cut only when no concrete version earns its place.
- **Reveal re-derivation:** after new information lands, one beat of processing is enough. Do not restate the same fact in two or three escalating paraphrases capped by a button line; the listener understood it the first time.
- **Named symmetry:** when two characters or situations mirror each other, let the recognition arrive as a specific, imperfect thought. One polished sentence stating the story's thematic equation (`cả hai chúng tôi đều...`, `hóa ra không phải chỉ mình...`) is the author speaking, not the character thinking.

## 5. Detail Originality And Example Immunity

Skill examples teach reasoning, not content. Never reuse their names, occupations, objects, gestures, sensory anchors, sentence skeletons, or before/after choreography merely because they are available in context.

Treat all examples as **contaminated** during drafting. Generate scene material from the current story only:

```text
Where exactly are they?
What were they already doing before conflict entered?
What objects must physically be there?
What time, money, body, work, family, or space constraint is active?
What would this specific POV notice for a practical reason?
```

Run the **object-presence test**. Before using an object to carry emotion, establish why it is present and being handled now. If the only answer is `để cho thấy cảm xúc`, remove it or redesign the action.

Common teaching-example residue to distrust—not ban—includes:

- straightening chopsticks or aligning objects to show control;
- a bowl of rice, soup, fish bones, or cooling food to show family feeling;
- placing keys, paper, a contract, or a phone on the table to make a line land;
- gripping an old phone, folding a page, reading a number twice or three times;
- a ceiling fan, refrigerator hum, door sound, rain, or flickering light inserted as generic atmosphere;
- an untouched meal, an extra place setting, or washed plate used as instant emotional shorthand;
- `lần đầu tiên`, `lần này`, `chỉ khác là`, or a final changed routine used to announce an arc.

Any one may be excellent when caused by this scene. Several appearing because they exist in skill examples create template prose.

Run the **example-distance test** before finalizing:

1. Identify every conspicuous object/action/image.
2. Ask whether it was independently derived from the scene map or remembered from instructions/examples.
3. Replace any borrowed noun-action pair with a consequence unique to the current setting and character.
4. Check that the replacement is not merely another stock symbolic object.

## 6. Character Life Without Stock Choreography

Humanity does not mean inserting domestic business around dialogue. A character feels alive through the interaction of:

- material conditions;
- private and public goals;
- uneven knowledge;
- history that changes present interpretation;
- competence and limitation;
- contradictions that affect choice;
- directional relationships in which each side wants, assumes, and gives differently;
- residue that changes later behavior.

Ordinary action is optional. Do not add food, cleaning, clothing adjustment, object alignment, or a household ritual to every emotional scene. Stillness and direct narration may be more truthful.

Do not turn profession, trauma, one habit, dialect, or one relationship signature into the entire person. A character can switch registers, behave inconsistently under new pressure, and surprise without violating their established range.

Relationships are directional. What A wants from B, assumes about B, and gives or withholds may not match B's side, and the same person shows a different self by listener, privacy, status, and stakes—in how much they explain, how directly they speak, which address form (`xưng hô`) they choose, and what they treat as needing no explanation. Prove this through behavior and address, not a relationship chart or a recurring prop; any shared phrase, object, or ritual is optional and must arise from this pair's world, never invented only to become a callback.

Trust and intimacy are earned in time, not granted by one scene. Access, testing, response, repair, and cost build understanding; shared danger or a single sharp conversation does not by itself prove two people know each other. Allow a character a motivated non-optimal choice when they believe it protects face, attachment, status, safety, or duty—keep the mistake consistent with their bias yet not inevitable, and never make a character foolish only to postpone an obvious solution.

Humanize opposing and minor characters without excusing harm. For an antagonist, separate the reason they tell themselves from their real benefit or fear, keep visible the alternative they rejected and the boundary they knowingly crossed, and let their ordinary role continue outside the conflict. For a minor character, supply only the one independent pressure, limit, or goal the interaction needs; do not build a full profile or use them as a plot vending machine.

Do not require every detail to change plot or symbolize theme. Some details locate body and world. But if a detail does neither orientation, character, atmosphere, comedy, pressure, nor consequence, it may still be expendable.

## 7. Prose And Vietnamese Semantic Judgment

Prefer natural Vietnamese collocation and thought order over source-language elegance. Preserve meaning, relationship temperature, and voice rather than translating an English metaphor or screenplay beat literally.

Audit:

- sentence says what happened before explaining its meaning;
- subject, action, and target are audible;
- abstraction is supported, not mechanically replaced by an object;
- image belongs to the POV and register;
- narrator does not over-certify an inference;
- narration does not explain that it is obeying a craft or knowledge limit;
- paragraph does not end by naming the theme already shown;
- short sentences are not used as automatic prestige;
- syntactic symmetry is not mistaken for emotional truth;
- concrete gesture is not repeated because it once worked elsewhere;
- action sentences do not all drag a trailing evaluative clause (`, khiến tôi...`, `, như thể...`, `, để lại...`); most actions may stand bare, with commentary reserved for the few places it changes meaning;
- soft-cliché vocabulary is distrusted at density, not banned singly: clusters of `khoảnh khắc`, `hành trình`, `chữa lành`, `bình yên`, `vụn vỡ`, `chông chênh`, `lặng lẽ`/`khẽ`, or `như một` + abstraction glosses make prose sound prepackaged;
- paragraph lengths vary across the page; a run of same-length paragraphs reads as machine cadence even when each is individually clean.

Professional prose can name emotion directly. `Tôi xấu hổ`, `anh ấy sợ`, or `tôi không muốn ở lại` may be more exact than symbolic choreography. Use showing, telling, summary, scene, image, and direct statement according to function—not as a hierarchy in which concrete action always wins.

## 8. Expert Editorial Passes

Run four passes in this order:

### Story truth

- events, choices, knowledge, time, and consequences hold;
- genre promise is paid;
- no skill template is visible as plot machinery.

### Human truth

- characters know only what evidence/history permits;
- reactions have individual timing;
- dialogue reflects formulation limits and separate voices;
- no instant intimacy, instant diagnosis, or universal eloquence.
- no guardrail leakage or disguised craft commentary appears in character voice.

### Prose truth

- Vietnamese collocation and register are natural;
- details arise from this scene rather than the skill's example bank;
- metaphors, motifs, and callbacks are optional and owned;
- plain sentences remain where they are strongest.

### Audio truth

- listeners can orient once without rereading;
- punctuation, speaker turns, names, and spoken tokens render cleanly;
- audio clarity does not flatten voice or force every sentence into the same length.

If a repair passes only the audio or technique layer but weakens story/human truth, reject it.
