# Romance

## Promise

Romance follows how people see, affect, and choose each other—not only whether they become a couple.

A working contract needs:
- people with goals beyond being loved;
- selective attention and mutual influence;
- a barrier whose removal has cost;
- earned access, trust, vulnerability, and repair;
- agency and consent for every key person;
- a relationship choice at the peak;
- an ending that fits HE, SE, BE, or open intent.

## Design Map

For each main person, know:
- goal outside love;
- need and fear inside the relationship;
- boundary and power position;
- how they give/receive care;
- what they misunderstand;
- what choice or change is required.

Chemistry must appear in behavior: noticing, responding, friction, shared activity, selective vulnerability, responsibility, and repair. Physical attraction or instant psychological diagnosis is not enough.

## Arc

Build changes in:
- access;
- closeness;
- trust;
- shared decision-making;
- cost of honesty or distance.

The arc need not be linear. A confession may increase closeness while reducing trust. A reunion must face who the people are now, not merely restore memory.

Misunderstanding lasts only when current evidence supports it, truth has a price, or the deeper barrier remains after explanation.

## Boundaries

Do not reward harassment, coercion, surveillance, control, or unilateral sacrifice as proof of love. A person may forgive without returning. Apology must address behavior and change; tragedy does not force forgiveness.

## Peak and Ending

The peak is the relationship choice under the highest established cost. Both sides need meaningful action.

- **HE:** show how the built problem will actually be lived with.
- **SE:** separation still completes understanding or choice.
- **BE:** tragedy follows planted choice/rule, not random accident.
- **Open:** close the present decision; leave only the future outcome open.

## Breakers

- characters exist only to complete each other;
- declared chemistry without mutual response;
- slow burn that is only delay;
- one conversation could solve everything but nobody asks without cost;
- sacrifice removes the other's agency;
- perfect therapeutic dialogue;
- love used to excuse abuse;
- kiss/reunion treated as solution to unresolved values or trust.
