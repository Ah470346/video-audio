# Audio-Specific Clarity and TTS Readiness

Use for cold listening and render-risk diagnosis.

## Cold-Listening Model

Assume the listener:
- cannot see quotation marks or paragraph design;
- cannot scan backward;
- may be multitasking;
- hears names, pronouns, numbers, and clues once.

A readable page may still fail in audio.

## Check

### Scene orientation
At meaningful scene changes, establish the needed combination of who, where, when, and continuing pressure.

### Names and pronouns
Avoid clusters of similar-sounding names, unexplained aliases, and long runs of same-gender pronouns. Re-anchor by name, relation, or title.

### Dialogue
Separate turns for pacing, but also run the strip-formatting test. Three or more speakers require stronger audible anchors. Test a dialogue-heavy sample before a long render.

### Sentence and paragraph rhythm
Flag overloaded comma chains, buried reveals, repeated low-information fragments, and identical sentence openings that sound like synthesis stutter.

### Spoken tokens
Decide the intended reading of dates, times, money, percentages, units, acronyms, account/legal codes, symbols, URLs, English words, brand names, and invented terms. Keep one form consistently.

### Formatting dependence
Verbalize information that exists only in tables, screenshots, color, indentation, footnotes, typography, or visual separators.

### Pure story text
Do not rely on SSML, `[pause]`, line-leading dashes, SFX/BGM, or headings unless production markup was explicitly requested.

## Readiness

- **Text ready:** coherent, natural, and pronounceable in principle.
- **Recording ready:** speaker turns and delivery pauses are clear for a narrator.
- **VoxCPM ready for sample:** punctuation, paragraphs, and token forms are clean.
- **Render verified:** representative dialogue/token-heavy audio has actually been listened to.

Block readiness when core chronology/speakers are unclear, an essential reveal depends on a likely token misread, or important pauses rely on unsupported formatting.
