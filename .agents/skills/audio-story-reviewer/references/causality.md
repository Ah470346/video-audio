# Causality, Continuity, Setup-Payoff, and Plausibility

Use this reference when the story feels random, convenient, contradictory, under-motivated, or when important payoffs arrive without preparation.

## Core Chain

Map the story as:

> Cause -> decision -> action -> consequence -> new decision.

Every major turn should be traceable through that chain. A scene may be quiet, reflective, comic, or atmospheric, but it should still change knowledge, intention, relationship, risk, or emotional state.

Chronology is not causality. `A rồi B` becomes causal only when A changes a belief, decision, opportunity, constraint, relationship, or physical state that helps produce B.

## Continuity Ledgers

Maintain concise ledgers while reviewing:

### Timeline

- event order;
- elapsed time;
- flashbacks and returns;
- deadlines, ages, pregnancies, travel time, healing time;
- day/night and weather when plot-relevant;
- whether characters could physically arrive or learn information in time.

### Knowledge

- what each character knows;
- when they learn it;
- from whom or what source;
- what they believe incorrectly;
- what they cannot know yet;
- whether narrator knowledge exceeds POV.

### Objects and Evidence

- object first introduction;
- owner or holder;
- physical location;
- condition;
- chain of custody;
- how it is found;
- what it proves and what it does not prove;
- payoff scene.

### Promises

- mystery questions;
- threats;
- goals;
- foreshadowing details;
- emotional promises;
- genre promises;
- title/opening promises.

### Goals

Track each consequential goal as:

| Owner | Goal | Why now | State | Attempt | Outcome | Next state |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Allowed states: active, blocked, achieved, abandoned, replaced, and forgotten.

- `Abandoned` is deliberate and has a visible reason or cost.
- `Replaced` has a trigger that makes a new goal more urgent or meaningful.
- `Forgotten` is an editorial defect: the story stops tracking a goal it taught the listener to expect.

When a distant goal or cause pays off after several scenes or boundaries, check whether a compact reminder is needed. Do not repeat the full setup; restore the smallest cue that reconnects the causal chain.

## Precise Defect Labels

Use these labels instead of vague "vô lý":

- **Missing cause:** an effect lacks a sufficient trigger.
- **Missing step:** the chain could work, but an essential action is skipped.
- **Contradiction:** new information conflicts with an established fact.
- **Convenience:** probability bends mainly to help the author.
- **Unpaid setup:** the story plants something and never uses it.
- **Unprepared payoff:** the resolution depends on late or unplanted information.
- **False stakes:** threatened consequences do not affect choices.
- **Causal redundancy:** multiple scenes repeat the same function.
- **Knowledge leak:** a character knows something not available to them.
- **Object teleportation:** an item appears, disappears, or changes condition without a path.
- **Reset consequence:** a cost or wound disappears after serving one scene.
- **Goal substitution without trigger:** a new objective silently displaces the active one.
- **Forgotten goal:** a tracked objective vanishes without achievement, abandonment, replacement, or deliberate deferral.
- **False causal connector:** words such as `vì`, `nên`, `do đó`, or `bởi vậy` assert a relation the events do not support.

## Setup and Payoff

A payoff is satisfying when the listener can look back and think, "I could have understood this, but I did not yet know its meaning."

Setups can be:

- visible clues;
- character habits;
- emotional wounds;
- rules of a world or family;
- repeated objects;
- rumors;
- failed attempts;
- overheard fragments;
- body language;
- earlier choices with costs.

Avoid:

- planting the exact answer so obviously that mystery dies;
- withholding all clue information until the reveal;
- using a new object, document, witness, or memory in the climax with no preparation;
- making the protagonist succeed because they suddenly remember a fact never shown.

Example:

Problem:

> Cô nhập sáu con số hiện lên trong đầu. Cánh cửa lập tức bật mở.

Diagnosis: unprepared payoff and missing causal step.

Repair options:

- plant worn keypad numbers;
- establish a meaningful date;
- give her partial code information earlier;
- make her infer from an established habit;
- redesign the climax around a prepared strength instead of the code.

## Coincidence Rules

Coincidence may create trouble. It should rarely solve trouble.

Acceptable uses:

- a chance encounter exposes a secret and worsens the problem;
- a storm traps characters together and forces a choice;
- a mistaken message creates pressure;
- a late arrival complicates the plan.

Weak uses:

- a stranger appears with the exact evidence needed;
- a villain confesses without pressure;
- an accident removes the antagonist;
- a forgotten document is found just because the ending needs it;
- the protagonist wins without choosing or sacrificing.

## Plausibility Versus Internal Consistency

Do not demand everyday realism from fantasy, horror, comedy, melodrama, fairy-tale logic, or rule-based premises. Demand that the story obey its own rules.

Ask:

- What has the story taught listeners to expect?
- What costs or limits exist?
- Does the exception have a reason?
- Do consequences persist?
- Do characters respond according to the world they believe they live in?

For reincarnation, time loop, system, or book-transmigration premises:

- define what the protagonist knows, remembers, and misremembers;
- identify whether future knowledge can be changed;
- track what the protagonist cannot know;
- require costs, risk, or limits for exploiting foreknowledge;
- prevent future knowledge from replacing present investigation and choice.

## Evidence and Proof

When a story uses documents, recordings, DNA tests, camera footage, contracts, medical reports, or witness testimony, check what the evidence actually proves.

Questions:

- Who created the evidence?
- Can it be forged, edited, misread, or challenged?
- Does it prove the conclusion or only raise suspicion?
- How did the protagonist obtain it?
- Why is it accepted by other characters?
- Is timing realistic for the genre?
- Does the antagonist have a plausible response?

Weak:

> Một bản ghi âm xuất hiện, mọi người lập tức tin cô.

Stronger direction:

> Establish who recorded it, why it survived, what exact claim it proves, and why the opposing side cannot dismiss it.

## Character Agency

A causally strong story gives the protagonist consequential choices.

Flag:

- protagonist repeatedly rescued by coincidence;
- side character solves the core conflict;
- antagonist withholds an easy victory without motive;
- information arrives passively at the exact needed time;
- heroine/hero reacts but rarely initiates;
- choices have no cost;
- failure does not teach or change strategy.

Repair direction:

- make the protagonist seek, trade, risk, lie, confess, sacrifice, confront, or choose;
- let failure create a new strategy;
- make evidence costly to obtain;
- make victory partial or morally complicated;
- make the antagonist respond intelligently.

## Scene Link Test

Between scenes, try to describe the link with one of these:

- **Therefore:** the prior result causes the next step.
- **But:** the next scene complicates or reverses the prior result.
- **Meanwhile:** a parallel strand changes stakes or information.
- **Because:** context explains an earlier result.

If the link is only `and then`, diagnose weak sequence or missing bridge.

Use a stricter bridge test when needed:

> Because of [prior event], [character] now believes/wants/can no longer [changed state], therefore chooses [next action].

If the sentence cannot be completed without inventing motive or information, the manuscript likely lacks a causal or motivational step.

Example:

Problem:

> Minh khóa cửa nhà kho, quyết định sáng mai sẽ báo công an.
> Ba ngày sau, Hạ đang đứng trước bàn thờ mẹ.

Possible bridge:

> Nhưng Minh không bao giờ kịp báo công an. Ba ngày sau, tin anh mất tích đưa Hạ trở về căn nhà cũ, nơi bàn thờ mẹ vẫn còn nghi ngút khói.

## Reporting Causality Issues

For each structural issue, include:

- exact scene or passage;
- defect label;
- missing link or contradiction;
- why the listener will feel cheated, confused, or unconvinced;
- smallest repair direction;
- whether the fix requires earlier planting, scene movement, changed motive, or a new consequence.
