# Horror

## Promise

Horror needs:
- something worth protecting;
- a threat shown through signs, intrusion, rule, or consequence;
- limited but progressing understanding;
- choices under fear;
- a price or changed meaning after confrontation.

Mystery is allowed; missing orientation is not. `Nothing is known` does not create fear by itself.

## Threat Map

Know privately:
- drive or repeated behavior;
- signs and pattern;
- scope and limits;
- what actions affect it;
- price of resistance or knowledge;
- what may remain unexplained.

Escalate by changing meaning, location, scope, target, or rule—not only loudness or graphic detail.

Sensory detail must locate danger, establish/break a pattern, provide evidence, or force action. Avoid symptom piles and generic darkness.

## Character Choice

Before a dangerous action, establish:
- what the character knows;
- what explanation they believe;
- what they lose by leaving or refusing;
- why the safer option is unavailable.

Do not make characters foolish simply to open a door. Fear may produce denial, checking, bargaining, obedience, flight, cooperation, or rule-breaking according to character.

## Rules and Dread

Rule horror must establish source, scope, evidence, conditions, and consequence. Rules cannot change only for the climax.

Waiting passages must shrink safety, clarify stakes, test a rule, force a choice, or reinterpret a sign. Repeating a sound with no change is delay, not dread.

## Peak and Ending

The peak is the terror set-piece where established capability, shrinking safety, rules, and cost converge with no easy retreat.

An open ending still states what the character chose and the current consequence. Leave only the threat's remainder open, not the story's debt.

## Breakers

- jumpscares that change nothing;
- gore replacing threat design;
- unseen entity assumed scary without signs/scope;
- character stays only because the script needs them;
- ten rules dumped before action;
- supernatural rule used to erase causality;
- mental illness treated as automatic danger;
- production tags used instead of prose;
- final ambiguity hides basic outcome.
