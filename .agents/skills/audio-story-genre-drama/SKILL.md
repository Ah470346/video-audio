# Relationship Drama

## Promise

Drama needs:
- a relationship worth losing;
- a truth, need, or power arrangement that cannot coexist with the current state;
- characters able to choose, resist, hide, leave, protect, or trade something;
- a consequence that changes trust, rights, distance, address, or daily life.

Depth does not require equal blame. Motive explains harm but does not cancel responsibility.

## Design Map

For each important relationship, track:
- what each side wants the other to do or recognize;
- fear, leverage, boundary, and old debt;
- information held or withheld;
- current `xưng hô` and likely shift;
- the choice that can permanently alter the relationship.

A third person belongs only when they change the force between the main pair.

## Secrets and Evidence

Use:

`need/pressure -> opportunity -> boundary-crossing choice -> self-justification -> consequence`

State what each clue/testimony/document actually proves and what it does not. Do not delay a clear truth merely to keep suspicion alive.

Choose the truth structure:
- discovery and verification;
- dramatic irony;
- known truth with consequence as the story.

## Scene and Peak

Active conflict may use:

`relationship pressure -> attempt -> response -> consequence`

Other scenes may absorb damage, reveal normal patterns, prepare a choice, or carry residue.

The peak is the confrontation or decision where strongest evidence, invested relationships, and least deniable choice converge. Consequences should land on-screen.

## Ending

Pay the relationship debt: leaving, legal action, boundary, reconciliation, changed duty, public/private truth, or refusal. Forgiveness does not reset history. Public humiliation and kneeling apology are not default payoff.

## Breakers

- twist replaces relationship;
- victim must be partly guilty to seem deep;
- backstory excuses harm;
- evidence proves more than its source allows;
- everyone becomes irrational to isolate the narrator;
- confrontation delayed by empty interruptions;
- viral-line dialogue;
- climax changes personalities rather than exposing planted choices;
- ending returns the relationship to its old state without cost.
