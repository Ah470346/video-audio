# Comedy

## Promise

Comedy is a character pursuing a real goal while a repeatable contradiction, blind spot, rule, or status problem produces escalating consequences.

A working contract needs:
- a recognizable expectation;
- a meaningful deviation;
- a frame safe enough for the intended target/audience;
- consequence and reaction;
- a specific comic POV.

Removing witty comparisons should not remove the comic engine.

## Comic Engine

Write one sentence:

`Whenever the character tries to [goal], [trait/rule] makes them [repeatable mistake or mismatch].`

Build characters from:
- image they protect;
- blind spot;
- real competence;
- threshold they cannot tolerate.

Competence keeps the story moving; blind spot creates the trouble. Do not use random stupidity.

Useful mechanisms: expectation mismatch, disproportion, escalating commitment, dramatic irony, status reversal, recognition, language, and callbacks. Choose by character and situation, not quota.

## Escalation

Use a causal snowball:

`small mistake -> character-driven fix/cover -> new obligation -> planted threads collide -> final choice`

Change axes: knowledge, promises, witnesses, status, publicity, cost of confession.

Misunderstanding needs a reason and price for not speaking. The world must remember bills, harm, authority, and previous lies.

## Set-Piece and Ending

One comic set-piece should let established threads collide in the same place with no retreat. Continue past the first laugh only through planted consequences, not fresh coincidence.

End with the promised comic release, status change, recognition, reconciliation, or satirical conclusion. Warm, sour, sharp, or open endings are valid.

## Boundaries

Keep harm and joke target aligned. Do not punch down at vulnerability, illness, poverty, accent, trauma, or lack of power. Dark comedy needs clear distance and target.

## Breakers

- quips in every sentence;
- narrator wit replacing situation;
- setup plus perfect counter-metaphor;
- one gag with no repeatable engine;
- punchline explained;
- punchline forced into unnatural final-word syntax;
- running gag repeated without changed function;
- severe harm treated as disposable joke;
- coincidence supplies each escalation;
- characters forget consequences after the laugh.
