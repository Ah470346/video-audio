# Audio Story Literary Texture

Make the prose sound as if this narrator selected it from this life and this moment. Do not make it “more literary” by increasing devices.

Literary texture is successful only when it improves POV, meaning, genre experience, memory, or audio rhythm at a cost lower than its clarity and artificiality risk.

## 1. Coordination

- Follow [../audio-story-engagement/references/phoi-hop-skills.md](../audio-story-engagement/references/phoi-hop-skills.md).
- Run only after action, motive, causality, and scene orientation are clear.
- Character and relationship life is base judgment, not a separate skill: build or repair it with the character-life gate in [../audio-story-engagement/references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md](../audio-story-engagement/references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md) (§6) before adding texture.
- Run the expert prose gate in [../audio-story-engagement/references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md](../audio-story-engagement/references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md).
- Run [../audio-story-engagement/references/ngon-ngu-con-nguoi.md](../audio-story-engagement/references/ngon-ngu-con-nguoi.md) for every conspicuous metaphor or high-impact dialogue cluster.
- Let the main genre control payoff and intensity.
- Add no device when plain prose already carries the scene.
- Never use texture to hide missing motive, evidence, mechanism, geography, or reaction.
- Never copy objects, gestures, occupations, images, names, or sentence shapes from skill examples.
- Run `audio-story-final-polish` last.

## 2. Texture Workflow

1. Lock POV, tense, narrator distance, and current emotional/genre function.
2. Read the scene without adding language; mark only where meaning, voice, or audio experience is generic or over-explained.
3. Build a compact perception profile.
4. Decide whether each marked place needs deletion, plain specificity, rhythm change, direct naming, image, contrast, omission, or nothing.
5. Add the smallest change.
6. Run device-function, semantic-fit, example-distance, and audio tests.
7. Remove visible technique and repeated patterns.
8. Return to plain prose wherever the device does not earn its cost.

Do not build a motif ledger, image field, or rhetoric inventory unless the existing story genuinely contains a recurring element worth controlling.

## 3. Perception Profile

Record only what affects prose:

```text
What this POV notices first now:
What they cannot or refuse to notice:
Relevant knowledge and its limit:
Dominant sensory/spatial bias in this scene:
Calm register:
Pressured register:
What they would say directly:
What they would only think or narrate later:
Images/comparisons they would not use:
```

Profession may affect precision and attention. It does not require occupational metaphor. Age, education, region, class, relationship, retrospective distance, and present control affect what wording is plausible.

## 4. Selection Before Device

Texture often comes from selection rather than figurative language:

- which fact appears first;
- what the narrator names or withholds;
- what physical relation in space matters;
- which sound or interruption changes attention;
- how much of a routine is summarized;
- where a sentence stops;
- whether emotion is named directly or left in consequence;
- which contrast is placed adjacent without explanation.

Prefer selection when a metaphor would only restate `sad`, `angry`, `afraid`, `beautiful`, or `broken`.

## 5. Image And Comparison Gate

An image or comparison may stay only when it has an exact mapping and at least one necessary function:

- characterizes the POV;
- compresses otherwise cumbersome meaning;
- makes an action or spatial relation clearer by ear;
- changes interpretation;
- creates genre-specific perception;
- carries established relationship history;
- prepares or transforms a genuinely recurring element.

Run:

```text
Literal target:
Source domain:
Exact mapping:
POV and register owner:
Narrative function:
Ordinary Vietnamese alternative:
What is lost if the image is removed:
Audio and category risk:
Did another speaker inherit the field?
Did the source come from a skill example?
```

Reject when:

- mapping is only `character has profession X -> life/love becomes X`;
- the source domain makes people or relationships obey object/system rules unintentionally;
- the image sounds natural in retrospective narration but not live dialogue;
- another character extends it for a perfect comeback;
- the same meaning survives more credibly in plain Vietnamese;
- a familiar symbolic object replaces actual behavior or consequence;
- the comparison needs explanation after it lands.

Do not stack source domains. Do not personify an environment unless observable behavior and POV projection both matter.

## 6. Image Fields, Motifs, And Callbacks Are Optional

Use an image field only when multiple independent moments already arise from the same lived environment or perception. `No governing field` is the default safe result.

Use a motif/callback only when its later return changes action, access, interpretation, relationship, or genre meaning. Recognition alone is insufficient.

Before keeping one, ask:

```text
Was the element physically and causally present before it became meaningful?
Would it exist if the writer had not planned a callback?
What changes on return?
Is the story already using another device for this meaning?
Does the narrator explain the changed meaning unnecessarily?
```

Do not convert every repeated object, phrase, sound, address form, or task into a motif. Do not schedule recurrences by count.

## 7. Omission And Earned Inference

Omit what listeners can infer from established behavior, context, relationship, or consequence. Do not omit identity, actor, object, mechanism, scene geography, or required causal premise.

Distinguish:

- emotional space from missing reaction;
- subtext from vague dialogue;
- bias from accidental confusion;
- provisional interpretation from narrator-certified truth;
- an unfinished thought from a sentence that simply lacks its subject or meaning.

Do not use omission to make a character appear deeper or more perceptive than the evidence permits.

## 8. Rhythm

Let rhythm follow attention, control, action, and emotional processing.

- Short sentences may mark impact, refusal, confirmation, or narrowed attention.
- Longer sentences may hold layered memory, accumulation, avoidance, or sustained observation.
- Fragments may work when context supplies the missing grammar and the voice owns them.
- Paragraph breaks may reset speaker, thought, scene, time, or emotional landing.

Do not associate shortness with quality or length with literariness. Avoid repeated cinematic fragments, identical three-part declarations, and a strong-short-line ending for every paragraph.

For VoxCPM, preserve an audible syntactic spine, natural breath points, clear subjects, separated speaker turns, and pronounceable tokens. Do not make visual poetry that disappears when heard.

## 9. Genre Calibration

Use the minimum texture that sharpens the main genre:

- **Drama:** relationship register, public/private contrast, consequence, and changed address; avoid symbolic household choreography.
- **Romance:** selective attention, adjustment, distance, vulnerability, and repair; avoid idealized catalogues and instant mind-reading.
- **Comedy:** timing, disproportion, material inconvenience, status, and consequence; avoid nonstop quips and caption voice.
- **Horror:** violated pattern, spatial/sensory information, unsafe familiarity, and rule consequence; avoid generic darkness, coldness, and unexplained sound.
- **Mystery:** precise discrepancies and observer bias; keep evidence literal and scope clear.
- **Speculative/premise stories:** let the lived world exceed interface vocabulary; do not turn every feeling into the mechanism.

Genre calibration never requires a metaphor, motif, sensory list, or special cadence.

## 10. Anti-Ornament And Anti-Imitation Pass

Flag:

- every scene ending with a thematic sentence;
- every emotion externalized through an object gesture;
- repeated household props or bodily micro-reactions;
- stock sounds/weather used as atmosphere;
- image fields selected from profession rather than perception;
- metaphor contagion across speakers;
- abstract reflection made “concrete” with an arbitrary object;
- three-part symmetry and `không phải... mà là...` used as prestige rhythm;
- repeated `lần đầu tiên`, `lần này`, `chỉ khác là`, or changed-routine announcements;
- skill-example nouns/actions/syntax appearing in the draft;
- prose that becomes more polished and less character-specific;
- prose that paraphrases a craft guardrail or explains why the narrator is not making a forbidden inference;
- direct emotional statements removed only because “showing” seems superior.

Repair hierarchy:

1. preserve story and human truth;
2. restore natural Vietnamese meaning;
3. remove borrowed/example-shaped material;
4. keep POV and genre function;
5. keep only the strongest necessary selection or device;
6. simplify audio syntax;
7. stop editing when the plain line works.

## 11. Device Function Test

For each noticeable device:

```text
Device or selection:
POV owner:
Function:
Human/genre value:
Clarity cost:
Artificiality/imitation risk:
Simpler alternative:
Decision: keep / simplify / delete
```

`Sounds beautiful`, `creates a motif`, `makes the line memorable`, or `matches the profession` is not sufficient alone.

## 12. Existing-Draft Repair

When prose feels generic or mechanical:

1. Diagnose whether the problem is missing human cause, generic selection, unnatural Vietnamese, repeated syntax, borrowed example material, or excessive device density.
2. Send missing human cause back to the base character-life gate ([../audio-story-engagement/references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md](../audio-story-engagement/references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md) §6); do not repair motive with metaphor.
3. Delete the most visible technique before adding anything.
4. Replace borrowed details with scene-derived consequences, not different stock symbols.
5. Restore direct emotional or factual naming where symbolic choreography obscures meaning.
6. Read the scene aloud and run final polish.

## 13. Output And Checklist

Do not label devices in the manuscript or show internal ledgers unless requested.

- [ ] Action, motive, causality, and orientation were clear before texture?
- [ ] The perception profile is limited to this POV and scene?
- [ ] Selection was considered before metaphor or motif?
- [ ] Every marked image has exact mapping, ownership, and function?
- [ ] No profession-to-metaphor shortcut or category mismatch remains?
- [ ] No other speaker inherited an image field for a comeback?
- [ ] Image fields, motifs, and callbacks were omitted when unnecessary?
- [ ] Omission preserves emotional space without hiding required information?
- [ ] Rhythm follows perception rather than a prestige template?
- [ ] Genre effect is sharper without technique becoming visible?
- [ ] All skill examples remained contaminated and absent from the draft?
- [ ] Stock domestic/object choreography was removed?
- [ ] Direct naming remains where it is the most exact choice?
- [ ] No craft rule or guardrail leaked into narrator/character wording?
- [ ] Vietnamese collocation, register, and one-listen audio clarity hold?
- [ ] Final polish will run after this pass?
