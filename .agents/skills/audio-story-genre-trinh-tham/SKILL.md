# Mystery

## Promise

A satisfying solution is surprising forward and reasonable backward.

The story needs:
- an investigable question;
- facts that change probability or interpretation;
- hypotheses that are tested and revised;
- a solution fitting timeline, motive, opportunity, method, and evidence;
- a reveal that changes judgment or action.

Fair play is required when the story promises a solvable whodunit. Procedural detail may be specialized, but the solution cannot rely on information created at the reveal.

## Design Backward

Lock privately:
- objective truth and action chain;
- motive and boundary-crossing choice;
- timing, access, and conditions;
- traces that must exist;
- traces that can be altered and what alteration leaves behind;
- mistake/unexpected factor;
- proof and rebuttal;
- consequence after truth.

Use safe narrative detail; do not provide actionable harmful instructions.

## Evidence and Hypotheses

For each important item, track:
- source and holder;
- time and chain of custody;
- what it proves;
- what it does not prove;
- whether it can be challenged or tampered with.

A clue may direct, constrain, contradict, link, reveal motive, reinterpret an old fact, or provide proof.

Investigation scenes should change a hypothesis, timeline, opportunity, motive, source reliability, or risk:

`question -> verification -> fact -> interpretation -> next action`

Mark fact, inference, and guess distinctly.

## Suspects and Interviews

Do not give every suspect the same board-game package. Side secrets must explain a lie, alter alliance, reveal motive, or change interpretation.

Do not use eye contact, shaking, verbosity, or posture as lie detectors. Verify content, timeline, access, interests, and contradictions. A slip matters only when the detail was genuinely unavailable to the speaker.

## Reveal and Ending

Pay four layers:
1. what happened;
2. which heard facts force the conclusion;
3. why the person chose it;
4. what knowing/proving it changes.

The peak may be reconstruction, verification trap, challenge, rescue, or choice about unusable truth. Avoid an uninterrupted solution lecture; allow intelligent objection and response.

## Breakers

- reveal introduces an untold decisive fact;
- clue proves more than its scope;
- culprit behaves irrationally only to hide twist;
- investigator ignores obvious checks for runtime;
- helper delivers the answer;
- jargon replaces reasoning;
- action stops the investigation rather than growing from it;
- emotional certainty treated as evidence;
- final solution does not change anything beyond naming the culprit.
