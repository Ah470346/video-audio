# Audio Story Genre: Drama

Drama keeps listeners through **shifting power and trust inside relationships**. A secret reveal matters only when it forces someone to choose and makes the relationship unable to return to its old state.

## 1. Coordination Contract

- Use `audio-story-engagement` as the base and follow [../audio-story-engagement/references/phoi-hop-skills.md](../audio-story-engagement/references/phoi-hop-skills.md).
- This skill defines the drama promise. It does not change approval gates, safety, Vietnamese address rules, or output format.
- If drama is secondary, use only the techniques that serve the main genre. Do not force adultery, public unmasking, or revenge.
- After drafting and self-checking, run `audio-story-final-polish` last.

## 2. Genre Contract

A relationship drama needs four parts:

1. **A relationship worth losing:** spouses, parent/child, siblings, close friends, long-time colleagues.
2. **A truth or need that cannot coexist with the current relationship state.**
3. **Power to choose:** the character must decide whether to speak, hide, stay, leave, protect, or trade something away.
4. **Relationship consequence:** after the choice, the way two people see, name, trust, or call each other changes.

Drama does not require "everyone is equally wrong." Sometimes one person clearly causes harm. Depth comes from motive, tactics, and credible consequences, not from assigning equal blame to the harmed person.

**Genre test:** if removing the secret/twist leaves the relationship with no goal, pressure, or choice, the story is only a sequence of events, not drama.

## 3. Central Question

Combine two halves:

> **What truth/power will be exposed, and what will the character choose after knowing it?**

Template:

> *When [truth] is revealed, will [character] choose [A] or [B], while [specific cost] is at stake?*

Vietnamese examples:

- *Khi biết chồng đã dùng tên mình vay nợ để cứu em trai, tôi sẽ tố cáo anh hay giữ im lặng để con không mất nhà?*
- *Khi di chúc cho thấy mẹ đã biết hai chị em bị tráo thân phận, ai sẽ công khai nó nếu sự thật tước luôn quyền thừa kế của người vô tội?*
- *Tôi có dám rời người luôn xin lỗi đúng lúc nhưng chưa bao giờ thay đổi?*

Do not default the question to "is there adultery?" You may confirm betrayal early and shift the question to why, who knew, what the character does, and what consequence is acceptable.

## 4. Relationship Map

For each main pair, record:

| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| Want | What does A want B to do or recognize? |
| Deep need | What does A truly need but has not admitted? |
| Fear | What does A fear losing if the relationship changes? |
| Leverage | Who holds money, information, status, affection, or decision power? |
| Boundary | What does A claim they will never accept? |
| Old debt | What past event prevents the present from being isolated? |
| Address form | Current `xưng hô` pair and likely shift point? |

### Conflict Triangles Only When They Matter

A third person matters only if they alter the force between two people: they hold information, are protected by one side, represent another path, or suffer consequences from the conflict. Do not add a lover or third party merely for jealousy; a child, debt, object, or mother can be the third point of a power triangle.

## 5. Scene Engine

Drama scenes run as:

> **relationship goal -> tactic -> reaction -> crack revealed -> power shift**

Example:

> The wife wants her husband to confess -> she asks around the hotel bill -> he blames her for being controlling -> he accidentally names the hotel before she has mentioned it -> she realizes the lie was prepared -> she stops asking for answers and starts collecting proof.

Common tactics:

- probing;
- soothing to obtain information;
- denying/changing topic;
- using duty or affection as pressure;
- bargaining;
- setting a boundary or exiting the game.

End each scene by changing at least one thing: who trusts whom, who knows what, who has leverage, which boundary was crossed, or which decision can no longer be withdrawn.

## 6. Betrayal, Secrets, And Evidence

Design betrayal as a causal chain:

```text
need/pressure -> opportunity -> boundary-crossing choice -> self-justification -> consequence
```

Motive explains behavior; it does not erase responsibility. Loneliness may explain why a person seeks intimacy outside marriage; it does not make betrayal correct.

### Three Truth Structures

1. **Discovery drama:** listener and character verify what happened together.
2. **Dramatic irony:** listener knows first; tension is when the character realizes and what they do.
3. **Consequence drama:** truth is clear from the start; the story is the aftermath and choices.

Evidence does not need to be ambiguous every time. Ambiguity is useful only when the character has a reason to test it. If truth is already clear, delaying proof can make the character seem artificially slow.

Use an evidence ledger:

| Evidence | Source | Actually proves | Does not prove | Who can rebut? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hotel photo | lobby camera | two people were present at the same time | their relationship | the photographed person |

Separate suspicion from conclusion. A test result, photo, message, or testimony carries only the weight its source and scope allow.

## 7. Character And Responsibility

The narrator does not have to be at fault in the tragedy. They need:

- clear want and boundary;
- a blind spot when appropriate;
- active decisions that change the story;
- the ability to misinterpret without hiding facts they obviously know just to deceive listeners.

For the harm-doer, write three layers:

1. what they tell themselves;
2. the real benefit they protect;
3. the boundary they knew about and still crossed.

Do not use children, elderly people, pregnant people, or victims only as stakes and then forget them. Give them reaction, need, and plot influence appropriate to their role.

Avoid false balance. Forgetting an anniversary is not automatically equal to forging documents. Compare responsibility by intent, power, ability to foresee harm, and available alternatives.

## 8. Confrontation And Dialogue

Subtext is a choice, not a quota. Use it when characters cannot speak plainly without losing power, evidence, or relationship. At the breaking point, one direct sentence can be stronger than endless circling.

Ask before confrontation:

- What does each person want the other to do right now?
- Who knows more, and who thinks they know more?
- What cannot be said in front of a third person?
- What makes the old tactic stop working?

Vietnamese examples:

**Too blunt, weak:**

> *“Anh là người phản bội và em rất đau khổ.”*

**Targeted probing:**

> *“Khách sạn gọi báo anh bỏ quên ví.” Tôi đặt ví của anh lên bàn. “Em tò mò không biết cái nào mới là đồ anh bỏ quên.”*

**Direct at the right breaking point:**

> *“Đừng xin lỗi nữa. Nói anh định sửa điều gì.”*

A memorable line must result from accumulation, not be pasted in as a slogan.

## 9. Escalation And Consequence Rhythm

Escalate through one or more axes:

- private -> family -> public;
- loss of trust -> loss of rights/property/custody;
- words -> signatures -> legal action;
- outsider -> closest family member siding with the opponent;
- "they lied to me" -> "what have I been living by?"

Not every segment must increase the level. The same stake can become closer, more concrete, or harder to avoid.

After a major reveal, let consequence exist:

> immediate reaction -> defensive action -> daily life changes -> new decision

A peaceful scene belongs only when it shows what can be lost or creates real contradiction, not as a checkbox "calm before storm."

## 10. Flexible Drama Structures

Choose by central question; do not force act ratios.

- **Discovery -> verification -> choice:** good for secrets/adultery; ensure investigation does not swallow the relationship.
- **Clear truth -> spreading consequences:** betrayal confirmed early; focus on who sides with whom and what cannot be repaired.
- **Both sides hide secrets:** each reveal changes judgment of the previous one. Keep first-person fairness; do not hide arbitrary facts.
- **Family ensemble:** one event passes through several pairs. Limit threads; each must affect the central decision.
- **Reconciliation drama:** climax is not punishment but saying/seeing what was denied. Reconciliation needs changed behavior and boundaries, not only an apology.

## 11. Hybrids And Premises

- **Drama + romance:** choose whether the main payoff is romantic relationship or broader truth/betrayal. Do not force HE.
- **Drama + mystery:** evidence must withstand testing; the solution must force a relationship choice.
- **Drama + comedy:** jokes must not erase consequences when drama is main.
- **Drama + horror:** fear should amplify loss of trust/home safety, not replace motive.
- **Drama + foreknowledge:** memory/book/system must change choices; do not repeat "I know this scene" at every step.
- **Drama + truyen-rac:** absurdity comes from consistent bias; the narrator still needs agency and the payoff still belongs to drama.

## 12. Repair Examples

### Event Only

> *Tôi thấy tin nhắn. Tôi rất đau. Tôi quyết định ly hôn. Anh hối hận.*

### Choice And Consequence

> *Tin nhắn xác nhận anh đã gặp cô ấy, nhưng người gửi lại là em trai tôi. Tôi có thể đưa điện thoại cho luật sư ngay tối đó. Thay vào đó, tôi gọi em đến vì chữ ký vay nợ dưới đoạn chat là tên của tôi. Đến sáng, chuyện tôi cần quyết định không còn là có tha thứ cho chồng hay không; là có tố cáo hai người đàn ông duy nhất con tôi gọi là gia đình hay không.*

The stronger version uses the reveal to change the goal and make the dilemma concrete.

### "Sympathetic" Antagonist Done Wrong

**Weak:** *Anh ngoại tình vì tôi bận chăm con, nên cả hai đều có lỗi.*

**Better:** *Anh cảm thấy bị bỏ lại và dùng cảm giác đó để tự biện hộ. Nhưng anh vẫn có những lựa chọn khác, và truyện để anh chịu trách nhiệm cho lựa chọn đã thực hiện.*

## 13. Common Traps

| Trap | Repair |
|---|---|
| Twist replaces relationship | After each reveal, force a changed choice/view/address form |
| Victim must be guilty to be "deep" | Give blind spot only if organic; avoid fake equal blame |
| Backstory excuses harm | Separate explanation from responsibility |
| Confrontation delayed by repeated interruptions | Make each delay produce a new decision or evidence |
| Universal evidence | State exactly what it proves and does not prove |
| Whole family acts irrationally to isolate narrator | Give each person separate interest, fear, and information source |
| Public unmasking by default | Choose payoff by character: legal, private, leaving, reconciliation, or public |
| Forgiveness = reset | Forgiveness does not erase boundaries or consequence |
| Dialogue is all viral lines | Each line is a tactic; keep resonant lines only when earned |
| Everyone changes personality after climax | Payoff comes from planted choices and preparation |

## 14. Checklist

- [ ] Does the contract include a relationship worth losing, a truth/need, a choice, and a consequence?
- [ ] Does the central question ask both "what is known" and "what is done after knowing"?
- [ ] Does each main pair have wants, fear, leverage, and boundary?
- [ ] Does each scene change trust, information, power, boundary, or decision?
- [ ] Does betrayal have need, opportunity, choice, self-justification, and consequence?
- [ ] Does the harm-doer have motive while remaining responsible?
- [ ] Is the narrator active without being forced into false blame?
- [ ] Does evidence prove only its actual scope?
- [ ] Does subtext appear as tactic, and direct speech at the right break?
- [ ] Does escalation follow meaningful axes rather than quotas?
- [ ] After major reveal, is there consequence and a new decision?
- [ ] Does the ending answer what the relationship becomes, not only who wins?
