# Character Voice, Lived Detail, and Emotional Residue

Use this reference when characters sound interchangeable, scenes feel sterile, or details appear selected by a generic writing system rather than by the current person and pressure.

## Voice Is More Than Vocabulary

For each major character, keep a compact fingerprint:

```text
Vocabulary range and social register:
Typical sentence length and directness:
What they name plainly:
What they avoid, soften, or cannot formulate:
How they lie or evade:
How they ask for help:
How anger changes their speech:
Public voice versus private voice:
Relationship-specific xung hô:
What changes under pressure:
```

These are tendencies, not catchphrase quotas. A voice may change with rank, intimacy, audience, exhaustion, fear, or strategic purpose.

## Swap Test

For consequential dialogue, ask whether another major character could say the line unchanged. If yes, check whether the line is intentionally neutral or whether age, knowledge, relationship, goal, and pressure should make it more owned.

Do not manufacture distinction through constant slang, accent spelling, verbal tics, witty metaphors, or exposition. Practical content, omission, timing, form of address, and what a person refuses to say often distinguish voice more reliably.

## Relationship-Specific Speech

A person does not use one voice with everyone. Track:
- who may interrupt whom;
- who uses names, titles, kinship terms, or pronouns;
- what can be said publicly but not privately, or the reverse;
- which old debt or power difference shapes politeness;
- what event would justify an address shift.

`Xưng hô` is an action in the relationship. A change requires a cause and should affect how the line lands.

## Lived Detail

Choose detail from the intersection of:
- the character's current goal;
- their role, work, money, body, status, and history;
- the physical place and available action;
- the relationship present in the scene;
- the consequence of what just happened.

A detail earns its place when it changes orientation, interpretation, blocking, risk, memory, relationship, or choice. Specificity does not mean inserting a phone, document, meal, key, rain, or body symptom into every scene.

## Uneven Human Attention

People do not observe scenes evenly. Under pressure, a character may:
- notice what helps or threatens the current goal;
- avoid the fact they cannot yet accept;
- fixate on a small practical task;
- misread a sign through an established fear;
- understand later rather than instantly;
- remember a detail because of shared history, not because it is symbolically elegant.

The narration may let the listener infer more than the character. Do not grant the narrator psychological certainty unsupported by evidence or history.

## Emotion and Residue

Emotion needs cause, personal meaning, and consequence. Choose the smallest truthful form:
- direct naming;
- thought;
- silence;
- speech failure or evasion;
- practical action;
- delayed reaction;
- a changed later decision;
- no added sentence when the event already carries it.

After a major emotional turn, track what remains:

```text
What belief or relationship changed?
What behavior is now harder or impossible?
What does the character avoid, seek, or decide differently later?
What physical, social, legal, financial, or domestic consequence persists?
```

Do not reset a character at the next scene merely to restore the planned plot.

## Anti-Sterile Checks

Flag when:
- every character speaks with the same polished intelligence;
- every feeling becomes hands, breath, heartbeat, or an object gesture;
- narration gives perfect psychological summaries at first contact;
- scene details could be moved to another story unchanged;
- characters answer each other with balanced slogans or matching metaphors;
- all reactions occur immediately and disappear afterward;
- a relationship is described but never changes selective attention or behavior.

Repair may be deletion, plain naming, a more exact fact, uneven response, a later consequence, or a relationship-specific line. It does not require more description.

## Audio Boundary

Distinct voice does not replace audible speaker anchoring. A listener should not need to identify a speaker by subtle style alone when names, relations, or a light attribution are needed. Preserve one-listen clarity while keeping character difference.
