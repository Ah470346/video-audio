# Core Standard for Vietnamese Audio-Story Prose

Use this reference during drafting, revision, final polish, and review. It contains the universal craft rules; genre skills should not repeat them.

## 1. Story and Human Truth

A scene must earn its place through at least one real function: choice, consequence, information, relationship change, pressure, atmosphere promised by genre, comedy, or emotional absorption. It does not need a visible beat template.

Keep a clear chain:

`trigger -> interpretation -> choice -> action -> consequence -> new state`

Coincidence may create trouble but should not solve it. Important outcomes must come from established access, ability, evidence, sacrifice, or choice.

Characters need:
- a goal beyond serving the plot;
- incomplete and uneven knowledge;
- competence plus limits;
- a reason for mistakes;
- agency that changes causality;
- consequences that remain after the scene.

Separate what a character **observes**, **infers**, **has repeatedly seen**, and **knows as confirmed truth**. One sharp encounter does not grant psychological omniscience or instant intimacy.

Motive explains behavior; it does not erase responsibility. Do not create false balance when power, intent, or harm is clearly unequal.

## 2. Natural Vietnamese, Not Visible Technique

Prefer natural Vietnamese thought order and collocation over translated elegance. State the event clearly before reaching for abstraction or theme.

A line may be direct, colloquial, lyrical, comic, blunt, or fragmentary. It is defective only when it damages meaning, relationship register, character voice, or intended rhythm.

Avoid recurring machine signatures:
- most paragraphs ending in a thesis, aphorism, twist tease, or fragment triple;
- repeated `không phải... mà là...`, `mãi về sau tôi mới hiểu`, `lần đầu tiên`, or symmetrical three-part sentences;
- a fresh reveal restated in several paraphrases;
- every emotion converted into hands, breath, heartbeat, food, keys, phones, documents, rain, or household objects;
- all characters speaking with the same polished intelligence;
- objects or professional vocabulary mechanically turned into metaphors for people;
- one speaker's metaphor followed by another speaker's perfect counter-metaphor;
- details copied from examples rather than caused by the current scene.

Skill examples are teaching material, never a content bank. Derive details from place, task, money, body, time, status, relationship, and immediate consequence in this story.

Direct emotional naming is allowed. `Tôi sợ`, `tôi xấu hổ`, or `tôi không muốn ở lại` may be stronger than symbolic choreography.

## 3. Dialogue as Human Contact

Keep direct dialogue only when the exact wording changes action, relationship, knowledge, humor, intimacy, misunderstanding, or consequence. Summarize greetings, logistics, repeated positions, and information both speakers know.

Before an important exchange, know:
- what each person wants now;
- what they avoid or cannot formulate;
- what each believes the other knows;
- what changes by the end.

Individual lines may be partial, practical, late, evasive, mistaken, or unanswered. Do not require a tactic, subtext, or quotable comeback in every turn.

Each voice must fit age, rank, education, region, relationship, public/private context, and current pressure. Vietnamese forms of address are relationship actions, not labels; a shift must have a cause.

Use [hoi-thoai-mot-giong-va-nhip-cau.md](hoi-thoai-mot-giong-va-nhip-cau.md) for single-voice speaker clarity, sentence landing, and TTS rhythm.

## 4. Emotion and Pace

Emotion needs a cause, personal meaning, and enough time to affect the next decision. Choose among direct naming, thought, silence, action, delayed reaction, later consequence, or no added sentence.

Do not use stock body symptoms as proof. A reaction should arise from the character's history, goal, role, and available action.

Pace is the rate of meaningful change, not sentence speed. Short sentences do not automatically create tension; long sentences do not automatically create depth. Vary narrative distance and sentence shape according to attention and pressure.

After a major reveal, allow the smallest believable processing beat before the character makes a new plan. Do not explain what the listener already understood.

## 5. Peak and Ending

Design one main peak where the strongest established threads converge under the highest cost and least room to retreat. Give it more space than ordinary scenes; do not cut away after the first impact.

The ending must:
- answer the central question;
- pay major promises and consequences;
- follow established choice and causality;
- leave the relationship/world in a changed state;
- avoid a moral summary or an unprepared final twist.

Minor threads do not all need symmetrical closure. A small honest remainder is allowed.

## 6. Compact Editorial Gate

Before finalizing, verify:

- Events follow choices and established rules.
- Characters know only what evidence/history permits.
- Each major character has agency and a distinct voice.
- Vietnamese wording and `xưng hô` are natural.
- Dialogue is worth hearing verbatim and clear by ear.
- Abstract or reflective lines have a clear literal proposition.
- Details arise from this scene, not a reusable template.
- Rhythm varies without becoming choppy or breathless.
- Main genre promise, peak, and ending are paid.
- No optional technique weakens the first eight items.
