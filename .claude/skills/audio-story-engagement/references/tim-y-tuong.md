# Step 0 — Research And Choose Story Ideas

This reference turns "look for trends" into a process with clear inputs, evidence, and outputs. The goal is not to copy popular stories; the goal is to detect **familiar pains, active motifs, and under-served angles**, then recombine them into new premises.

> **A trend is a signal, not a complete idea.** A rising hashtag does not prove listeners want a long story about it; a comment-heavy post does not represent the whole audience.

## 1. When To Research

Use web research when the user:

- wants new/hot ideas for a platform or audience;
- has not locked a premise and only gives genre/theme;
- wants to use a current social, professional, local, or technology phenomenon;
- needs to check whether a motif is saturated or fact-sensitive.

Do not over-research when:

- the user already gives a specific premise and wants execution;
- the story is fully fictional and does not need current signals;
- web noise will not change the creative decision.

Even with web data, do **not** assume "hot" means "suitable." The idea must survive through conflict, choice, and consequence after the trend fades.

## 2. Build A Search Brief

Before opening the browser, write one line:

> **Audience + platform/format + genre + target emotion + length + region/language + time window**

Vietnamese example:

> *Người nghe Việt 25–45 tuổi, truyện audio YouTube 20–30 phút, drama gia đình, muốn ức chế rồi hả hê, tín hiệu 90 ngày gần nhất tại Việt Nam.*

Without a brief, the agent may mix unrelated "high view" items and propose ideas for the wrong audience.

Distinguish:

| Type | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Topic** | What field is the story about? | inheritance, adultery, AI voice cloning |
| **Motif** | What familiar conflict machine? | contract marriage, identity swap, locked room |
| **Premise** | Who must do what, why is it hard, what is lost if they fail? | A wife must prove the "husband" call was fake before her mother-in-law transfers all assets |

Web often supplies topics/motifs. The agent must build premises.

## 3. Search Signals At Three Layers

Each research pass should use at least two layers, preferably from independent source types.

### Layer A: Expressed Demand

Purpose: what people search, watch, or discuss.

- Google Trends: compare topics by region/time.
- TikTok Creative Center/Trends: hashtags, related videos, audience, region.
- YouTube/Spotify/podcast charts or search if format-relevant.
- Public comments: identify questions, reactions, pains; do not treat as representative statistics.

### Layer B: Durable Pain And Conflict

Purpose: separate the currently visible from what people emotionally care about.

- Forums/confessions: repeated dilemmas and unsaid things.
- Articles/interviews/research: real consequences, professional process, insider language.
- Trope discussions: familiar expectations and what audiences are tired of.

Do not take a real case as plot. Extract only the **abstract conflict pattern**: power imbalance, secret, choice, cost.

### Layer C: Distant Inspiration

Purpose: avoid all ideas matching top results.

Examples:

- family drama + voice authentication;
- rental-room horror + hospital shift handover;
- reunion romance + copyright dispute;
- mystery + local wedding ritual.

Near sources support accuracy and expectation; distant sources can create novelty. Keep a distant source only if it creates understandable conflict.

## 4. Query From Six Angles

Do not search only "story ideas." Create 2-4 purposeful queries:

1. **Signal:** `site:ads.tiktok.com creative center [topic] Vietnam`
2. **Pain:** `[topic] dilemma regret conflict`
3. **Mechanism:** `[job/technology] common failure fraud verification`
4. **Genre expectation:** `[trope] readers tired of / subvert`
5. **Ignored POV:** `[event] from the perspective of [secondary character]`
6. **Distant source:** `[topic] ritual procedure protocol psychology`

Example for a twin drama:

- `twin identity fraud real world verification`
- `chị em ruột tranh chấp thừa kế tâm sự`
- `twin swap trope readers tired of`
- `wedding objection from event photographer perspective`

## 5. Read Trend Data Correctly

### Google Trends

- 0-100 is normalized relative interest, not absolute search volume.
- Data is sampled; low-volume terms can show 0.
- Compare the same region, window, and search type.
- Use Topic rather than a single term when possible, but inspect its scope.
- A short news spike may not support a long story. Compare 7 days, 90 days, and 12 months.

### TikTok Creative Center

- Filter by region, industry, and time.
- Look beyond hashtag rank: trendline, related videos, audience, co-occurring hashtags.
- A hashtag may be driven by music, memes, or ads rather than storytelling demand. Note why it is rising.

### Triangulation Rule

Call a signal "worth trying" only when at least two evidence types support it:

- **Quantity:** trend line, count of videos/posts, frequency.
- **Quality:** comments/questions/reactions show emotional conflict.
- **Durability:** appears across weeks/months or ties to durable pain.

With only one source, label it a **hypothesis**, not "hot."

## 6. Distill Without Copying

Use source cards, not plot copying:

```text
URL / date viewed:
Observed signal:
Abstract conflict:
Verifiable real detail:
Absolutely do not copy:
```

Do **not** copy names, places, event sequence, distinctive lines, identifying details, twist, or ending from one source.

### Three Meaningful Transformations

A premise inspired by a real case should change at least three meaningful axes:

- central POV: victim -> witness; bride -> photographer;
- conflict mechanism: adultery -> identity fraud; money -> custody;
- setting/power: rich family -> small cooperative; city -> tourist town;
- moral choice: expose -> protect an innocent linked person;
- outcome: revenge -> self-release; arrest -> costly reconciliation.

Changing names and places while keeping the whole event sequence is not new creation.

Protect real people:

- Do not speculate about guilt or mental health of identifiable people.
- Do not use private details, personal confessions, or minors as identifiable material.
- For sensitive events, synthesize from multiple sources and fictionalize deeply; use official sources when accuracy matters.

## 7. Idea Generator By Recombination

Create enough raw seeds before choosing the 3-5 shown to the user. For open briefs, try roughly 6-12 seeds. For narrow briefs, 3-5 truly distinct seeds may be enough. This is a working range, not a quota.

Fill six slots:

> **familiar pain + character wants + new mechanism + motivated resistance + hard choice + concrete cost**

Example:

> *Sợ bị thay thế + cô dâu muốn giữ quyền làm mẹ + giả giọng AI + em song sinh muốn giành lại cuộc đời từng bị cướp + công khai bằng chứng hay bảo vệ bí mật về đứa trẻ + mất hôn nhân hoặc mất quyền nuôi con.*

### Anti-Sameness Matrix

Vary:

| POV | Arena | Secret type | Reward structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| harmed person | family | identity | public reversal |
| accomplice | workplace | finance | escaping control |
| witness | wedding | blood relation | saving someone but losing honor |
| seeming antagonist | hospital | false memory | truth costs both sides |

If four proposals only change job/name while keeping POV, secret, and ending, they are one idea.

Deepening questions:

- Why can the character **not leave immediately**?
- What does the antagonist gain and how do they justify themselves?
- Without the twist, is the conflict still worth listening to?
- Which choice changes, not merely which information changes?
- Does the cost touch what the character values most?
- Can this mechanism be understood through audio without diagrams?

## 8. Score And De-Duplicate

Score each idea 0-2:

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | generic | unusual | one sentence creates a concrete question |
| Motivation | character dragged | vague want | clear want and urgent reason |
| Causality | random events | some links | choices cause next consequence |
| Cost | only "sad/hard" | some loss | concrete escalating loss |
| Difference | copied trope | changed setting | changed POV/mechanism/choice |
| Audio viability | needs visuals | tellable | object/dialogue/sound can carry it |
| Expansion | one scene only | short story | enough for 3 acts without filler |
| Safety/ethics | exploits real harm | repairable | clearly fictional/responsible |

Prefer ideas scoring around 12/16 or higher with no 0 in motivation, causality, or safety. Scores do not replace judgment; they force the agent to name why an idea works.

Quick duplicate check:

> `central role / goal / mechanism / choice / promised result`

If three of five slots match, merge or redesign.

## 9. User Approval Format

Show only the **3-5 strongest ideas**, each in this format:

```markdown
1. **Tên/hook tạm:** ...
   **Logline:** [nhân vật] phải [hành động] trước khi [hậu quả], nhưng [lực cản].
   **Điểm khác:** góc nhìn/cơ chế/lựa chọn làm ý này khác trope quen.
   **Cảm xúc hứa hẹn:** người nghe sẽ đi qua ... rồi nhận ...
```

Then ask:

> *Bạn muốn triển khai ý nào, hay trộn/chỉnh điểm nào?*

**Stop at the approval gate.** Do not write the story, detailed outline, or Central Question before the user chooses, unless they explicitly authorize the agent to choose and proceed.

Do not dump the full research log. Mention only 1-2 source signals if they help the user choose.

## 10. Complete Example

Input:

> *Tìm ý tưởng drama về chị em song sinh, đăng YouTube audio, không muốn mô-típ đổi chỗ cưới nhau quá cũ.*

Extracted signals:

- Durable pain: sibling comparison, right to recognition, family favoritism.
- Modern mechanism: voice cloning, identity verification, facial data.
- Stale element: twin pretends to be bride to steal husband; everyone fails to notice too long.
- Distant source: evidence workflow of an event photographer.

Three distinct premises:

1. **Đoạn ghi âm không thuộc về cô dâu**
   **Logline:** Một cô dâu mang thai phải chứng minh đoạn ghi âm thú tội ngoại tình là giọng AI trước giờ ký thỏa thuận hủy hôn, nhưng người duy nhất giữ bản gốc lại là em song sinh đã biến mất.
   **Điểm khác:** xung đột nằm ở bằng chứng và lòng tin, không phải tráo người.
   **Cảm xúc hứa hẹn:** bị cả nhà nghi ngờ -> tự giành lại quyền kể câu chuyện của mình.

2. **Người chụp tấm ảnh cuối cùng**
   **Logline:** Nhiếp ảnh gia phát hiện cô dâu trong album và cô gái bước vào lễ đường không có cùng một vết sẹo; để cứu hôn lễ, anh phải thú nhận mình từng giúp người em tạo hồ sơ giả.
   **Điểm khác:** kể từ người đồng lõa, mỗi bằng chứng cũng buộc anh trả giá.
   **Cảm xúc hứa hẹn:** tò mò điều tra -> day dứt đạo đức -> chuộc lỗi có giá.

3. **Người em không muốn làm chị nữa**
   **Logline:** Sau nhiều năm bị gia đình ép đóng thế chị gái hoàn hảo, người em chủ động công khai mọi lần tráo vai đúng ngày chị được thừa kế, dù sự thật ấy cũng khiến chính cô mất quyền sở hữu công ty đã gây dựng.
   **Điểm khác:** “phản diện” từ chối vai bị áp đặt; mục tiêu là thoát danh tính, không cướp hôn phu.
   **Cảm xúc hứa hẹn:** tức giận -> đảo góc nhìn -> giải thoát pha mất mát.

All three use "twins" but differ in central role, mechanism, choice, and reward.

## 11. If Web Is Unavailable

- Say clearly that no current trend data was accessed; do not pretend to have "caught trends."
- Use genre knowledge to create diverse seeds, score them, and pick 3-5 ideas through the same process.
- Label the output: *"Các ý dưới đây dựa trên mô-típ bền và phán đoán sáng tác, chưa được kiểm chứng bằng xu hướng hiện tại."*
- Keep the approval gate unless the user authorized choosing.
