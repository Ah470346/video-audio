# Base Safety And Sensitive Content Editing

Use this reference while ideating, drafting scenes, and reviewing final audio/video stories for platform publication. The goal is to tell clearly, with weight and responsibility; do not reduce safety to synonym-swapping for filters.

> **There is no absolutely safe word.** Platforms evaluate context, focus, tone, realism/graphicness, purpose, and packaging. Replacing *"giết"* with *"đoạt mạng"* does not make a graphic scene safe.

## 1. Base Principles

### Evaluate Scenes, Not Keywords

For each risky passage, answer six questions:

| Variable | Question |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Is this fictional, educational, news, endorsement, or instruction? Does context appear in the story/video itself? |
| **Focus** | Is the scene focused on harmful action, or consequence, decision, and people? |
| **Tone** | Condemning, neutral, empathetic, sensational, pleasurable? |
| **Graphicness** | Does it describe wounds, fluids, pain, or method too specifically? |
| **Realism** | Is the act easy to imitate, presented as real, or tied to real people/events? |
| **Subject** | Are there minors, identifiable victims, sexual violence, or vulnerable groups? |

A direct neutral word in a news-like sentence may be lower risk than a chain of euphemisms that makes the act more lurid. Do **not** label any Sino-Vietnamese word, legal term, or distorted spelling as something "AI usually misses."

### Weight Comes From Consequence, Not Gore

To make a scene hurt more, clarify:

- Who loses what?
- Who chose and pays?
- Which object, habit, or relationship changes meaning?
- What does the character do immediately after shock?

Do not intensify by replacing a direct verb with a "cold feeling" phrase while keeping the same graphic detail.

### Important Context Must Be In The Content

For high-risk content, a description-line disclaimer may not be enough. Let the narration/video show that the scene is fictional, critical, or consequence-focused. Do not insert awkward disclaimers into the story; embed context through genre, framing, and point of view.

Vietnamese example:

> *Trong bản cung, đó là chi tiết cuối cùng người điều tra xác nhận. Còn với tôi, nó chỉ là khoảnh khắc cả gia đình không thể quay lại như trước.*

This keeps focus on investigation and consequence, not enjoyment of harm.

## 2. Scene Risk Levels

### Level 0 — Ordinary

Verbal conflict, betrayal, loss, illness, or dangerous action mentioned briefly without graphic detail.

**Handling:** write naturally. Do not avoid all direct words until the event becomes unclear.

### Level 1 — Sensitive But Non-Graphic

Threat, aftermath of violence, victim discovery, accident, funeral, abuse mentioned without detail.

**Handling:** emphasize reaction, choice, consequence; limit duration; avoid repeated shock.

### Level 2 — Distribution/Monetization Risk

Prolonged threat, domestic violence, specific injury, blood, weapons, sexual assault, self-harm, or realistic dangerous behavior.

**Required handling:** reduce detail, do not describe method, do not romanticize, embed context, review title/thumbnail/audio. Consider a content note if format fits.

### Level 3 — Do Not Publish As Is

- Instruction, encouragement, challenge, or glorification of harm.
- Description that helps recreate self-harm, weapons/substances, or evading rescue/investigation.
- Extended focus on wounds, bodies, pain, or sexual violence for shock.
- Blaming, humiliating, or identifying real victims.
- Sexual content involving minors.

**Handling:** remove or redesign the scene. Do not try to save it with synonyms.

## 3. Seven-Step Editing Process

1. **Mark risky scenes.** Include narration, dialogue, title, on-screen text, subtitles, sound, and planned visuals.
2. **Name the dramatic function.** What must this scene prove: danger, loss, clue, cruelty, relationship turn?
3. **Keep function, cut excess.** If a sentence only increases luridness without changing understanding/choice, cut it.
4. **Shift focus.** From method to prior sign, moment choice, witness reaction, or long-term consequence.
5. **Check imitability.** Remove dose, sequence, precise location, tools, and evasion tips when they become instructions.
6. **Read aloud.** Catch euphemisms that are more graphic than the direct version, or avoidance that makes the event unclear.
7. **Check current platform policy.** Policies change; this file does not guarantee distribution or monetization.

Sentence repair formula:

> **minimum event needed + observable reaction + consequence that changes the scene**

Vietnamese example:

> *Khi cửa kho mở, tôi biết ông đã không còn sống. Mẹ tôi không khóc; bà chỉ tháo chiếc nhẫn cưới và đặt lên bàn viên điều tra.*

Listeners understand the event; emotion is in the mother's action, not body detail.

## 4. Self-Harm And Sexual/Domestic Violence Protocols

### Self-Harm / Suicide

Do not:

- describe method, tools, dose, location, or sequence;
- frame the act as beautiful revenge, proof of love, victory, or inevitable escape;
- use romanticized imagery like "final peace" or "complete release";
- reduce cause to one event or one person;
- use self-harm as a shock twist and ignore aftermath.

Do:

- emphasize warning signs, intervention, support, recovery, and impact on survivors;
- leave characters with choices, listeners, and help pathways;
- if death is unavoidable in fiction, state briefly without method;
- for real-world crisis content, add country-appropriate support resources in publication context; do not invent hotline numbers.

### Sexual Assault And Domestic Violence

- Do not use assault as seasoning to make a villain worse.
- Do not describe bodies/acts in eroticized or extended ways.
- Do not require victims to be "perfect" to be believed.
- Avoid blame questions about clothes, timing, alcohol, or not fighting back.
- Give victims later agency: whether/when to speak, whom to seek, what boundary to set, what legal/social consequence follows.
- If there is no room to handle aftermath, choose another form of harm instead of exploiting assault superficially.

## 5. Strong Writing Without Graphic Detail

Safer sources of weight:

1. **Object evidence:** warm cup, call still connected, shoes in the wrong place.
2. **Aftershock behavior:** wiping the table three times, folding a shirt, refusing to say a name.
3. **Absence:** empty seat, unanswered habit.
4. **Costly decision:** sign, tell truth, keep secret, leave, stay.
5. **Concrete consequence:** custody loss, family split, changed testimony, unrepaired trust.

Vocabulary principle: clarity first, reduce graphicness second. Use this table as voice guidance, not filter-evasion:

| Purpose | Direct neutral | Indirect but clear | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| confirm death | *đã chết, không còn sống* | *bác sĩ xác nhận ông không qua khỏi* | extended symptom/body sequence |
| mention violence | *bị hành hung, bị bạo hành* | *trên tay cô còn dấu của trận đòn tối qua* | describing each blow/wound |
| mention weapon | *con dao, khẩu súng* when needed | *vật trong tay hắn* if identity not important | damage mechanism |
| mention assault | *bị xâm hại/cưỡng ép* | *điều xảy ra trong căn phòng đó không có sự đồng thuận* | body/act detail |
| mention self-harm | *đã cố tự làm hại mình* | *đêm ấy cô rơi vào một cuộc khủng hoảng và cần cấp cứu* | method/tools/dose |

If indirect wording makes listeners misunderstand the event, use direct neutral wording. **Vagueness is not safety.**

## 6. Title, Thumbnail, Captions, Sound

- Title should promise the story accurately; avoid glorifying, joking about, or exploiting victims.
- Do not use thumbnails simulating wounds, self-harm method, blood, or suffering moments for clicks.
- Subtitles and on-screen text count as content; do not distort spelling to evade scanners.
- Sound can make a non-graphic scene sensational: avoid extended pain sounds, body sounds, or repeated effects.
- If using content notes, be specific but not sensational: *“Tập có đề cập bạo lực gia đình và khủng hoảng tâm lý.”*

Weak title:

> *Cô gái tự kết liễu để trả thù chồng phản bội*

Better:

> *Bức thư để lại khiến người chồng phải đối mặt với bí mật của mình*

The second keeps drama promise without framing self-harm as victory or bait.

## 7. Before/After Examples

### Synonym Does Not Fix Scene

**Weak:**

> *Hắn đoạt mạng cô bằng ba nhát dao rồi nhìn dòng đỏ tràn qua nền gạch.*

Problem: `đoạt mạng` is not safer than `giết`; the scene still focuses on method and graphic image.

**Repair direction:** name the death clearly enough for the listener, then shift to a consequence that belongs to the established people and circumstances. Do not use an untouched meal, covered pot, table setting, or waiting household object as automatic grief shorthand.

### Keep Event Clear, Shift To Consequence

**Weak:**

> *Sau nhiều năm bị đánh đập, cô đau đớn đến mức không thể chịu nổi nữa.*

**Repair direction:** move from the harm to a credible act of documentation, help-seeking, protection, boundary, or exit appropriate to the character's access and risk. Let the concrete consequence carry agency without a stock key/door image or a `lần đầu tiên` thesis.

### Self-Harm Is Not Decorative Twist

**Weak:**

> *Cô chọn cái chết để anh phải ân hận suốt đời.*

**Better:**

> *Khi nhận ra cô đang ở trong khủng hoảng, người em gọi cấp cứu và ở lại bên cửa cho đến sáng. Cuộc hôn nhân vẫn tan vỡ, nhưng câu chuyện từ đó không còn là chuyện trả thù; nó là hành trình cô học cách tin rằng mình còn một tương lai khác.*

### Horror Without Body Detail

**Weak:** long description of injury shape.

**Better:**

> *Tôi không nhìn vào trong vali lần thứ hai. Tôi chỉ nhận ra chiếc đồng hồ của bố đang chạy ở đáy, dù ông đã mất tích mười năm.*

## 8. Publication Checklist

- [ ] Sensitive scene has clear dramatic function, not shock only.
- [ ] Listener understands the event without method/graphic detail.
- [ ] Content does not instruct, endorse, romanticize, or challenge harmful behavior.
- [ ] Victim has humanity, choice, and consequence; not just a prop.
- [ ] Minors and sexual violence handled with highest caution.
- [ ] Title, thumbnail, captions, and sound do not sensationalize beyond narration.
- [ ] Necessary context appears in the content, not only description.
- [ ] Platform policy checked at time of posting.
- [ ] No claim that a word is definitely safe or a filter-evasion trick.
