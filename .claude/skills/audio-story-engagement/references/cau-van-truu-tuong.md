# Over-Abstracted Sentences And The Concrete-First Contract

This reference defines the over-abstraction defect class (`câu trừu tượng hóa quá mức`), the two mandatory tests that catch it, the positive repair pattern, and the mandatory clarity-check subagent cadence during drafting. It applies to **every sentence of every story**, not only closers or reflective lines: event narration, inner thought, foreshadowing, and reveals are all in scope.

The user-facing rule this encodes: audio stories exist for listeners to enjoy and understand in one pass, not to study like literature. A sentence the listener must decode is a defect no matter how deep it sounds.

## 0. The Defect Is Topic-Independent

This defect is defined by the **shape of the sentence, not its subject**. It appears in every genre and topic: horror, romance, mystery, comedy, family drama, revenge, historical, fantasy, workplace, coming-of-age. The debt/marriage examples throughout this file are one illustration only; the identical shape must be caught about a ghost, a first kiss, a murder weapon, a childhood memory, or a business deal. Never treat a sentence as safe because its topic differs from the examples, and never narrow the check to legal/financial language. The two tests in section 2 operate on any sentence regardless of subject — they are the universal detector; the examples are just recognizable shapes.

The same shape across genres:

> **Horror —** *"Đêm đó tôi hiểu ra cái tên của thứ vẫn luôn ở lại trong căn nhà cùng chúng tôi."* → *"Đêm đó tôi nhận ra tiếng bước chân trên gác không phải của bố. Bố đã mất ba năm trước."*
>
> **Romance —** *"Anh trao cho tôi điều mà cả hai chúng tôi đều không dám gọi tên."* → *"Anh nắm tay tôi trước mặt cả cơ quan, dù biết ngày mai ai cũng sẽ bàn tán."*
>
> **Mystery —** *"Sự thật cuối cùng là một cánh cửa dẫn tới nơi tôi chưa từng dám nhìn vào."* → *"Người tráo lọ thuốc không phải y tá. Đó là con gái của bệnh nhân."*

## 1. The Defect Class

A sentence is over-abstracted when the listener must reconstruct **who did what, to whom, with what consequence** because the concrete event has been replaced by concept nouns or a pseudo-philosophical shape. This is a class judged sentence by sentence, never a banned-phrase list. It applies to any subject; the illustrations below happen to use a debt drama.

Recognizable shapes (one family, not separate rules):

- **Concept nouns replacing the event:** `văn bản`, `món nợ`, `quyền sở hữu`, `sự thật`, `lựa chọn`, `số phận`, `ý nghĩa`, `tổn thương`, `sự phản bội` standing in for an action the story could simply name.
- **Pseudo-philosophy (`triết luận giả`):** the sentence has the silhouette of an insight but cannot be restated as exactly one plain literal sentence. Shapes: `Tôi mới hiểu tên của một điều không nói cho tôi biết...`, `Có những thứ tưởng là... nhưng hóa ra...`, `Tôi đã trao đi điều mà chính mình không biết...`, `Một tờ giấy đã lấy khỏi tôi thứ tôi từng nghĩ thuộc về mình...`
- **Personification that hides the human cause:** `Tờ giấy đã cướp căn nhà của tôi` when the truth is `Chồng tôi dùng chữ ký của tôi để thế chấp căn nhà`. Imagery may dramatize; it may not misattribute agency.
- **Author voice over character voice:** inner language above the character's education, situation, and emotional state. A woman losing her house thinks `Tối nay mẹ con tôi ngủ ở đâu?`, not `Tôi đang đánh mất quyền sở hữu tài sản vì hệ quả của một văn bản pháp lý.`
- **Idea-stuffing:** document type + debt + perpetrator + legal consequence + emotion + philosophy packed into one sentence.

Production example of the defect and its repair, from this repo's own catalog:

> Defect: *"Đến khi người ta niêm phong nhà, tôi mới biết tên của một văn bản không nói cho tôi biết mình đã nhận món nợ nào, đã giao tài sản nào cho ai."*
>
> Repair: *"Đến khi người ta niêm phong nhà, tôi mới biết mình đã ký gánh những khoản nợ nào cho ai, đã ký thế chấp căn nhà này cho ngân hàng từ bao giờ."*

The repair keeps the retrospective ache and the ten-year irony; it loses only the decoding work.

## 2. Two Mandatory Tests

Run both on every sentence that reaches for depth, summary, or metaphor:

- **Literal-translation test (`dịch sang nghĩa đen`):** restate the sentence's concrete proposition — what happened, was perceived, was said, or was decided — as one plain, non-literary sentence. If that proposition cannot be recovered, or the restatement must invent a missing actor/action/object/consequence, the sentence is defective. Several possible motives, suspects, symbols, emotional readings, or future explanations are allowed when the surface proposition is clear.
- **Dishwashing test (`nghe khi đang rửa bát`):** would someone washing dishes or driving understand the basic actor/action/object/orientation on first hearing? They do not need to solve a mystery, decode subtext, understand a symbol, or know an intentionally withheld answer immediately.

These tests protect literal clarity, not interpretive uniformity. Direct emotional naming, efficient telling, deliberate genre ambiguity, fair clue withholding, subtext, character-owned metaphor, researched slang, `nói lái`, and emotionally open resonance are not defects when the concrete proposition is audible. Do not flatten them merely to produce one interpretation.

## 3. The Positive Repair Pattern

Repair by making the event audible, never by deleting depth or swapping one abstraction for a prettier one:

1. Write the literal event first: **character + direct verb + object + consequence** (`ký giấy bảo lãnh`, `thế chấp căn nhà`, `gánh khoản nợ`, `giấu hợp đồng`, `sang tên tài sản`, `lấy chữ ký`, `đuổi khỏi nhà`) — not concept-noun paraphrases (`nhận lấy một món nợ`, `trao đi quyền sở hữu`, `đối diện với sự thật`, `đánh mất điều thuộc về mình`).
2. Split when one sentence carries several facts: `Đó là giấy bảo lãnh. Tôi ký để chồng vay tiền. Anh không trả được thì tôi phải trả thay. Căn nhà này là tài sản bảo đảm.`
3. Earn the general sentence: a philosophical or summarizing line may stand only **after** the concrete event immediately before it has made the literal meaning obvious. `Tôi ký giấy bảo lãnh cho chồng mà không đọc. Đến khi người ta niêm phong nhà, tôi mới hiểu: có những chữ ký chỉ mất một giây, nhưng phải trả bằng cả một mái nhà.`

Chất văn is not the enemy. A good sentence has two layers: layer one, the listener instantly knows what happened; layer two, they feel the residue. Only layer two without layer one is the defect. Deliberate metaphor whose literal meaning is unambiguous and whose voice belongs to the character is not a violation; do not flatten the narrator into a police report.

## 4. Mandatory Clarity-Check Subagent Cadence

Detection is delegated so the drafting voice cannot grade its own homework:

- **During drafting (Step 4):** after completing each segment of roughly **500-1000 words**, at a sentence boundary, send that segment's text to the clarity-check subagent — `audio-story-clarity-check` in `.claude/agents/` for Claude Code, `audio_story_clarity_check` in `.codex/agents/` for Codex. Validate each finding against full context, genre, approved intent, voice, deliberate withholding, and active trend/texture choices. Apply every **confirmed** repair before drafting the next segment; reject false positives instead of forcing a rewrite.
- **Before final polish:** after all drafting and literary-texture changes, run the subagent once on the full assembled draft. Repair every confirmed defect, then proceed to `audio-story-final-polish`.
- The subagent reports findings in fixed blocks (`ORIGINAL / ISSUE / WHY IT'S UNCLEAR / LITERAL MEANING / REWRITE`). It is a narrow read-only detector, not an authority over genre, emotion technique, clues, voice, slang, or final wording. The main agent stays the author: it may use, reshape, or reject a suggestion after context validation, and every accepted repair must still pass both tests in section 2.
- If the subagent is unavailable in the current environment, run sections 1-3 of this reference manually on each segment at the same cadence, and say so in the wrap-up report.
