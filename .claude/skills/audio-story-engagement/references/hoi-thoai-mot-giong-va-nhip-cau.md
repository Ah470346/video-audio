# Single-Voice Dialogue and One-Listen Rhythm

Use for all genres written for one narrator or one TTS voice.

## 1. Audio Cannot See Formatting

Listeners cannot rely on quotation marks, indentation, or paragraph alternation. For each important dialogue cluster, mentally remove visual formatting and ask:

- Who is speaking each consequential line?
- Can ownership be known without counting alternating turns?
- Does a third speaker, interruption, long turn, topic shift, or narrator insertion break the pattern?
- Could the line plausibly belong to either speaker?

If ownership becomes uncertain, add the smallest audible anchor.

Useful anchors:
- name, relation, or title;
- distinctive `xưng hô`;
- a purposeful action that only that speaker performs;
- a light attribution;
- a narrator bridge;
- information or diction only that speaker owns.

Do not tag every line. A clear two-person exchange may run without tags. Re-anchor at actual risk points, not after a fixed number of turns.

Action beats must clarify speaker, blocking, subtext, silence, access, or consequence. Decorative gestures added only to identify a speaker create mechanical prose; use a light tag or compress the exchange instead.

In scenes with three or more speakers, anchor more often than page fiction would require.

## 2. Dialogue Value

Keep exact speech when wording itself matters. Convert to narration when an exchange only handles:
- greetings or logistics;
- repeated arguments;
- facts both people know;
- procedural explanation;
- filler between decisive lines.

A short, tense exchange can remain rapid when each voice has an audible marker. One-word answers and fragments are valid when their owner and function are obvious.

## 3. Semantic Landing

A sentence must land on a meaning the listener can recover in one hearing. Flag endings that stop on an under-resolved placeholder or unnatural collocation, such as a vague `đường`, `thứ ấy`, `chuyện đó`, `một điều`, or abstract noun whose concrete stake was never established.

Test:
1. What exactly did the speaker mean?
2. Can the sentence be restated plainly without inventing a missing actor, object, or consequence?
3. Is the ambiguity intentional and supported, or merely unfinished wording?

Repair by naming the specific result, object, action, or consequence in the character's natural vocabulary. Do not replace one vague abstraction with another smoother abstraction.

Fragments are allowed when context completes them. Do not turn every clipped line into formal full grammar.

## 4. Sentence Rhythm

Short sentences work for impact, refusal, confirmation, shock, narrowed attention, or decisive action. They become mechanical when many nearby lines share the same low-information shape:

`subject + small action`
`subject + small action`
`subject + small action`

Repair only the weak run:
- combine related actions;
- omit repeated subjects;
- summarize routine movement;
- connect an action to choice or consequence;
- vary sentence length at natural thought boundaries.

Do not lengthen every short sentence. Preserve the strongest impact lines.

Also check consecutive sentences with identical openings. Keep deliberate repetition only when it builds pressure; otherwise vary the first word or sentence route so TTS does not sound stuck.

## 5. Pause and Spoken Form

Use:
- commas for short breaths;
- periods for complete thought stops;
- paragraph breaks for speaker/thought/scene resets;
- question marks for real questions;
- ellipses only for genuine trailing or hesitation.

Paragraph breaks help pacing but do not prove speaker ownership.

Avoid relying on decorative dashes, repeated ellipses, `[pause]`, SSML, SFX/BGM, or layout unless production markup was requested.

Write consequential numbers, dates, times, acronyms, symbols, codes, and foreign words in the form listeners should hear. Use one consistent spoken form for recurring terms.

## 6. Cold-Listening Gate

Before approval, read or simulate the passage continuously:

- Scene entry names who, where, and when when needed.
- Pronouns have one audible antecedent.
- Similar names and same-gender characters are re-anchored.
- Every important speaker turn is identifiable.
- Sentence endings carry a complete intended meaning.
- No choppy action-report run or overloaded breath remains.
- The listener can follow facts without scanning backward.
- Voice and subtext remain intact; clarity has not flattened the prose.
