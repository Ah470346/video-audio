# Audio Story Engagement

Turn every story request into a Vietnamese audio script that is understandable in one listen, driven by motivation, causality, emotional progression, and TTS-friendly sentence rhythm. Do not merely list events; design expectation, presence, and payoff so the listener wants to remain with the story. Do not claim that wording can reliably "trigger" dopamine, oxytocin, cortisol, mirror neurons, or similar biological effects. Treat those labels only as old mnemonics; the practical writing operations live in [references/ky-thuat-chi-tiet.md](references/ky-thuat-chi-tiet.md).

**Supreme goal of this entire skill suite:** a story a Vietnamese listener experiences as told by a human expert — natural, alive, and worth retelling. Every rule in every audio-story skill serves that goal. When mechanically complying with any individual rule would make the prose sound less human, choose the more human option, within safety, logic, and one-listen clarity. Rules are instruments; the reader's felt experience is the measure.

When this skill runs with genre, premise, or final-polish skills, follow the shared priority contract in [references/phoi-hop-skills.md](references/phoi-hop-skills.md). Specialist skills must not skip approval gates, loosen safety rules, impose hard quotas, or change a user-approved creative intent.

**Core test:** every section must offer a reason to remain with the story—progress, consequence, recognition, presence, humor, atmosphere, or emotional processing. Do not manufacture a hook, reversal, or quotable line every minute. If a section offers no value to the whole, rewrite or cut it.

Run the shared expert-quality gate in [references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md](references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md) throughout planning, drafting, and revision. Human truth, earned knowledge, natural Vietnamese, and original scene-derived detail outrank visible technique.

## Project Defaults

Apply these defaults unless the user asks otherwise. Direct user requirements for point of view, setting, naming system, ending, or style override defaults within the boundaries of safety, logic, and one-listen clarity.

### 1. Pronouns, Address, And Setting

Write in **first person** by default. **The default story world is modern China** in the register Vietnamese listeners know from translated fiction (`truyện dịch/convert`): Chinese-style names, Chinese-leaning institutions and daily life, generic or Chinese-flavored locations (*thành phố A, tập đoàn Lục thị, khu chung cư cũ*). Never use Vietnamese place names, Vietnamese-coded institutions, or a Vietnamese-coded setting — and only switch to a Vietnam or other-country setting when the user explicitly requests it.

- Build Vietnamese address forms (`xưng hô`) pair by pair. Consider relative age, kinship/rank, power, intimacy, public/private context, and current emotion. Kinship labels often anchor family relations in narration; names are natural in dialogue when needed for clarity, contrast, or a relationship temperature shift.
- Default names: Chinese-style Sino-Vietnamese names (`tên phong cách Trung Quốc/Hán-Việt`) with common Chinese surnames and preferably two-syllable given names, e.g. the structure of *Lâm Vân Khê, Cố Thừa Ngôn*. Follow convert address conventions on top of Vietnamese pronouns: intimate address uses `tiểu/A + tên` (*tiểu Nhã, A Vãn*) or the full two-syllable given name; people who are not close use the **full surname + given name**; social contexts use surname + title (*Cố tổng, bác sĩ Thẩm*). Do not address a character by a bare one-syllable given name (*"Nhã ơi", "Hân à"*) — that is Vietnamese register and breaks the world. Keep Vietnamese pronouns; do not import `ta-ngươi` into modern stories. Use natural Vietnamese names only when the user asks for a Vietnamese setting. The naming system must match the story world. Never copy sample names from the skill into the actual story.
- Ear clarity beats anti-repetition. Use name, relation, title, pronoun, or omitted subject according to point of view. In scenes with multiple people, repeat names/roles when a pronoun could be ambiguous.
- A change in address form is a relationship beat: *anh-em -> tôi-anh*, given name -> title, intimate -> distant only after an action/emotion makes the shift meaningful.
- For pair maps, default naming, ambiguous pronouns, and audio-first examples, read [references/xung-ho-dat-ten.md](references/xung-ho-dat-ten.md).

### Semantic Clarity Before Compression

Listeners may infer emotion, motive, subtext, theme, and the answer to a deliberately opened mystery. They must not have to guess basic orientation that the prose owes them: who/what a pronoun denotes, who acted on which object, where a consequential person/object is, which prior premise licenses a connector, how a premise-critical action works, or which established rule caused an effect.

- Every `nó`, `họ`, `người đó`, `việc ấy`, `chuyện này`, `thứ kia`, and omitted subject must resolve to exactly one audible antecedent. A frame, message, input box, comment, and hidden system are different entities; name each role before compressing to a pronoun.
- Audit presupposition words: `chỉ là`, `nhưng`, `vì vậy`, `vẫn`, `cũng`, `lại`, `nữa`, `mỗi lần`, and `lần này` may be used only when the listener has already heard the statement, cause, state, or prior occurrence they depend on. Do not use a connector to pretend missing setup exists.
- Establish the method the first time a premise-critical verb appears: how the narrator writes, hears, sees, activates, deletes, changes, or controls something. Later mentions may compress only after the mechanism is stable.
- Audit abstract evaluations and narrator meta-commentary, including `an toàn`, `có đời sống`, `chân thật`, `có trọng lượng`, `có chiều sâu`, `đủ thật`, or `có ý nghĩa`. A grammatical judgment is still unclear if the listener cannot state **what exact target is being judged, what the criterion means here, and which audible detail proves it**. Unpack the judgment into the missing word, behavior, contrast, or consequence; do not merely replace it with a smoother abstract adjective.
- Reject **over-abstracted sentences** (`câu trừu tượng hóa quá mức`) anywhere in the story, not only in closers: any sentence where concept nouns (`văn bản`, `món nợ`, `quyền sở hữu`, `sự thật`, `lựa chọn`, `ý nghĩa`) or a pseudo-philosophical shape replace the concrete event, so the listener must decode who did what, to whom, with what consequence. Every sentence reaching for depth must pass the literal-translation test (`dịch sang nghĩa đen`: exactly one plain restatement) and the dishwashing test (understood on first hearing while doing chores). Repair with **character + direct verb + object + consequence**; a general or philosophical line may stand only after the concrete event immediately before it made the literal meaning obvious. Full class, tests, and repair pattern: [references/cau-van-truu-tuong.md](references/cau-van-truu-tuong.md).
- Deliberate ambiguity offers multiple meaningful interpretations supported by the text. Missing identity, action, geography, mechanism, or causality is not mystery; it is missing information.

Use [references/ro-rang-mot-luot-nghe.md](references/ro-rang-mot-luot-nghe.md) for the referent test, connector/presupposition table, abstract-evaluation test, required-inference boundary, mechanism examples, and cold-listening gate. This gate is mandatory for system/foreknowledge stories, action scenes, evidence reveals, climaxes, endings, and any sentence that remains grammatical but makes a listener ask `nó là gì?`, `ai làm?`, `an toàn khỏi điều gì?`, or `vì sao lại thành thế này?`.

### 2. Firm Modern Prose, Not Sentimental Prose

Default style: fewer words, clean sentences, concrete details, emotion revealed through action. Sentimentality (`sến`) kills appeal faster than a weak plot.

- Cut rather than decorate. Description may orient space/body, establish lived normality, carry atmosphere or comedy, sharpen character, create pressure, or cause consequence. It need not advance plot or symbolize theme, but it must earn its cost in this story. If it exists only to sound beautiful or sad, delete it.
- Avoid stale emotional metaphors: no *tim tan vỡ, lệ rơi như mưa, đau như dao cứa, thế giới sụp đổ*. Use direct naming, behavior, image, or summary according to the scene. Do not automatically replace every feeling with a domestic object gesture.
- Do not whine or over-explain grief. Modern characters often swallow feeling; one small action should let listeners feel it themselves.
- One precise detail is better than three pretty details. Reduce adjectives/adverbs; keep strong nouns and verbs.
- Test every sentence that sounds like `bolero` melodrama. If it could be pasted into ten other stories unchanged, make it specific.
- Concrete wording must also fit the human target. Do not treat a marriage, person, memory, or moral choice as a broken object, machine, account, case file, or interface merely because the POV has that profession or the story uses that motif. Marked analogies need character ownership and an exact mapping; otherwise use natural human Vietnamese.

Vietnamese calibration examples must stay Vietnamese:

> *"Nước mắt tuôn như mưa, tim tan nát"* -> *"Tôi không khóc. Tôi chỉ thấy tay mình lạnh."*
>
> *"Ánh mắt trìu mến vô bờ"* -> *"Anh nhìn tôi lâu hơn bình thường đúng một giây."*

### 3. Narrator-Led By Default, Dialogue As Impact

For single-voice audio/TTS stories, use the narrator as the spine: summarize routine exchanges, connect choice to consequence, guide psychology, and push plot. Direct dialogue is scarce. A scene does not need dialogue unless the exact wording matters. If the user asks for audio drama, scripted dialogue, or multiple actors, treat that as a different mode, but still judge value and pressure across the exchange rather than assigning a tactic to every line.

- Keep direct speech when the wording itself matters to action, relationship, voice, misunderstanding, humor, intimacy, or consequence. Judge the exchange, not every line: a character may hesitate, answer partly, speak plainly, or fail to formulate the real issue without each utterance functioning as a tactical move.
- Compress procedural communication: greetings, logistics, repeated arguments, known information, and plot explanation should become narration or be cut. Do not narrate an idea and then make a character repeat it.
- Narrate psychology with epistemic discipline: separate observation, provisional interpretation, repeated pattern, and confirmed truth. A sharp stranger or skilled professional cannot infer a hidden lifelong habit from one convenient line unless later evidence earns it.
- Vary narrative distance according to scene need; do not cycle mechanically through summary -> concrete detail -> inner logic -> dialogue -> consequence. Do not turn the story into a flat report, but do not stage or decorate every beat either.
- Write the speaker's immediate human intention in plain Vietnamese before shaping subtext. Do not optimize dialogue for a memorable quote, viral caption, symmetrical comeback, or `mic-drop` ending. Resonance is a possible result of pressure, not a line requirement.
- Reject **aphorism tennis**: one speaker makes a polished metaphor and the next instantly extends or defeats it in the same semantic field. This usually means both characters are serving the author's theme instead of responding as separate people.
- A character's job may shape attention without controlling all their emotional vocabulary. Do not let a carpenter make every relationship into furniture, an accountant make every feeling into a ledger, or another speaker borrow that field only to cue a clever rebuttal.
- For dialogue selection, narrative distance, and editing examples, read [references/ky-thuat-chi-tiet.md](references/ky-thuat-chi-tiet.md#10-narrative-voice-dialogue-and-subtext).
- For marked metaphors, occupational language, over-finished speech, and category mismatch between objects and human relationships, run [references/ngon-ngu-con-nguoi.md](references/ngon-ngu-con-nguoi.md). This gate is mandatory for every conspicuous metaphor and high-impact dialogue cluster.
- Treat every example inside a skill as contaminated teaching material. Do not reuse its names, objects, gestures, occupations, sentence skeletons, or domestic choreography in the story. Generate detail from the present scene and run the example-distance test in [references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md](references/van-xuoi-chuyen-nghiep.md).

### 4. VoxCPM/TTS-Ready Sentence Breaks And Dialogue Pauses

All completed stories are audio/TTS-bound by default. Write punctuation and paragraphing as a performance map for VoxCPM, while keeping the saved file pure story text.

- Use commas for short breaths, periods for complete thought stops, question marks for real questions, and paragraph breaks for thought turns, speaker turns, scene shifts, or emotional resets.
- Do not rely on visual-only formatting, decorative dashes, raw symbols, or untested production tags for pauses. Do not insert SSML, `[pause]`, SFX, or BGM unless the user explicitly requests production markup.
- Shape direct dialogue as speaker turns: one speaker turn per paragraph in multi-speaker exchanges, closed quotation marks, terminal punctuation inside the spoken line, and action beats only when they clarify speaker, subtext, or silence.
- Split dialogue that cannot be said in one natural breath. Use sentence breaks and meaningful action beats instead of making one quoted line carry confession, explanation, reaction, and consequence.
- Write important numbers, dates, times, acronyms, and symbols in the form listeners should hear, especially for VoxCPM-bound scripts.
- Watch consecutive sentences that open with the same words while drafting. A render pipeline never reorders sentences, so two same-opening sentences always play back to back regardless of chunk size, and a chunk-based renderer resets prosody at the start of each chunk, so the repeat reads as the synthesizer stuttering rather than a device. If the repetition is deliberate, give each sentence a different **first word** (drop the subject, front an adverb, invert the clause) while keeping the shared anchor phrase; inserting a short unstressed word after the same first word (`Tôi muốn` → `Tôi rất muốn`) is not enough — production listening showed the ear swallows the insert and still hears a stutter.
- For detailed pause hierarchy, Vietnamese dialogue handling, VoxCPM token risks, and examples, read [references/voxcpm-tts-ngat-nghi.md](references/voxcpm-tts-ngat-nghi.md).

## Mandatory Workflow For Story Creation

Do not start drafting the story immediately. Run these steps in order. Steps 0 and 2 are **user-approval gates**: writing any episode or chapter prose before both gates have been explicitly resolved with the user in this conversation is a workflow violation, even in autonomous or non-interactive mode.

### Step 0: Research And Propose Ideas (Hard Gate — User Chooses)

Before writing any story prose, first: (1) research current motifs/topics for the requested genre, (2) distill them into **3-5 clearly different ideas**; each idea must include a hook/title, a 1-2 sentence logline, and a twist/angle, (3) present the numbered list and **stop for the user to choose**. Discussion is part of this gate: answer questions, revise, merge, or replace ideas on request, and re-present until the user explicitly picks one.

This gate may be skipped ONLY when the user's request contains an explicit no-approval instruction, such as *"không cần hỏi"*, *"không cần duyệt"*, *"cứ viết luôn"*, or *"tự quyết hết, viết thẳng"*. Nothing else waives it. In particular, none of the following counts as authorization to skip: a detailed premise (genre, character types, length, episode count); phrases like *"bạn tự tìm ý tưởng"*, *"làm phong phú thêm cốt truyện"*, or *"sáng tạo thêm"* — these mean research and PROPOSE, then stop; an autonomous session; genre/premise skills being active; or approval granted for a previous story. When skipping under an explicit no-approval instruction, briefly state the chosen direction outside the story file, then continue.

Read [references/tim-y-tuong.md](references/tim-y-tuong.md) for the research method, output format, and fallback when web access is unavailable.

### Step 1: Central Question

Lock one central question that listeners want answered from the first minute and that is fully paid off at the end. It must include personal stakes: who loses what, not just what information is missing.

### Step 2: Opening Selection Gate (Hard Gate — User Chooses)

Before writing the real story, choose the opening type with the user. Use [references/mo-dau.md](references/mo-dau.md) to select the 3-4 best opening techniques from the 10-type menu, then draft **3-4 short opening options**, each using a different technique and each providing transformation, minimal anchor, specific gap, and movement. Label each technique, show the actual draft text of each option, present them, and **stop for the user to choose**. Discussion is part of this gate: the user may mix options or request a new direction; re-present until they explicitly pick.

This gate may be skipped ONLY when (a) the user already gave a concrete opening line/paragraph to use, or (b) the user's request contains an explicit no-approval instruction (*"không cần hỏi"*, *"cứ viết luôn"*, ...). A chosen idea from Step 0 does not waive this gate — approving the plot is not approving the opening. When proceeding under (b), briefly note the chosen technique and reason outside the story file, then continue.

### Step 3: Expectation, Clarity, And Causality Map

Before drafting, outline the central question/goal, required subloops, where loops open/progress/close, the choice -> consequence chain, information/emotional/power rewards, and setup for twists. Also record stable names/roles for any mechanism or easily confused entity, the method of premise-critical actions, and the explicit premise required by each major turn. Do not impose fixed numbers of loops or reversals.

Name the story's intended **peak scene** and run the retell test from the Peak Design section below. If no planned scene passes, redesign the outline before drafting.

### Step 4: Draft

Draft using the 10 core techniques summarized below plus VoxCPM/TTS-ready sentence breaks and dialogue pauses. Expand the user-approved Step 2 opening into the actual opening of the story.

**Mandatory clarity-check cadence:** after completing each segment of roughly 500-1000 words, at a sentence boundary, run the `audio-story-clarity-check` subagent (Claude Code: `.claude/agents/audio-story-clarity-check.md`; Codex: `.codex/agents/audio-story-clarity-check.toml`) on that segment's text. Treat each returned `REWRITE` as an advisory repair: validate the finding against full context, genre promise, approved intent, character voice, deliberate withholding, and any approved trend/texture; apply every **confirmed** repair before drafting the next segment, and reject false positives rather than flattening the passage. After all drafting and literary-texture changes, run the subagent once more on the full draft before Step 6. The main writing agent owns the final wording. Details and fallback when no subagent runtime is available: [references/cau-van-truu-tuong.md](references/cau-van-truu-tuong.md).

### Step 5: Self-Check

Run the checklist below and self-repair before returning or saving.

### Step 6: Mandatory Final Polish

After completing the draft and the base/genre/premise checklists, use `audio-story-final-polish` to repair causality, motivation, plot depth, emotional reaction, dialogue, Vietnamese address forms, pronouns, read-aloud rhythm, and VoxCPM/TTS pause readiness. Keep the narrator as the spine; do not add extra back-and-forth just to make scenes "lively." This must be the final content pass. If any story content changes afterward, run `audio-story-final-polish` again from the beginning.

### Step 7: Save The Script

Save complete scripts under `/Users/truongdv/Documents/projects/video-audio/kich-ban/` in the folder for the main genre: `drama/`, `trinh-tham/`, `kinh-di/`, `tinh-cam/`, or `hai-huoc/`. Hybrid stories go under the main genre. Premises such as `xuyên sách`, `hệ thống`, `trùng sinh`, or `truyện rác` do not get their own folders.

- File name: `ten-viet-thuong-khong-dau.md`, words separated by hyphens, e.g. `chong-toi-ngoai-tinh.md`.
- The saved file must contain **pure story only**: start with the first hook sentence and end with the final story sentence. Do not include title headings, metadata, genre/POV/word-count notes, read-aloud instructions, SSML, `[pause]`, `---`/`***` act separators, `[SFX]`/`[BGM]`, or chapter labels unless the user explicitly requests production cues. Put production notes, if any, in a separate response or file.

## 10 Core Techniques

This is the quick operating version. For examples, edge cases, and deeper tables, read [references/ky-thuat-chi-tiet.md](references/ky-thuat-chi-tiet.md).

1. **Hook and promise.** Open with a concrete change, enough anchor for listeners to know what they are missing, and a push into action. Match the title; do not use false hooks or shock stacking. For opening menus and transmigration/reincarnation openings, read [references/mo-dau.md](references/mo-dau.md).
2. **Open loops.** Each loop needs a specific question/goal, a reason to care, progress path, and intended closure. Use loop count according to length. Provide real progress instead of endless interruption.
3. **Variable rewards.** Vary reward type: information, capability, emotion, power reversal, confirmation, or fair surprise. Rewards must be earned through choice, investigation, or tradeoff.
4. **Immersive detail.** Choose senses and reactions according to what the character wants/notices. Prefer action that shows emotion. Do not replace every feeling with a stock body symptom.
5. **Audio pacing.** Each scene or compact passage needs value and relation to the whole, but not a visible `goal -> obstacle -> turn` formula. Movement may be decision, consequence, accumulation, recognition, atmosphere, comic experience, or credible emotional delay. Control pace through events, distance, rhythm, punctuation, paragraphing, dialogue, and information load.
6. **Setup-payoff and "Aha!".** Design backward from payoff to required clues. Surprise must be hard to predict but explainable in retrospect, and it must change decision/relationship, not merely provide trivia.
7. **Emotional investment.** Give characters specific wants, inner conflict, agency, and individual detail. Understand antagonist motives without excusing responsibility.
8. **Escalation and release.** Increase cost, proximity, deadline, irreversibility, or relationship pressure. Quiet scenes must still create consequence, alliance shifts, clues, or decisions.
9. **Sound as information** when production layers are requested. Use sound to locate, signal change, or create motif. In pure narrated stories, write sound into action; do not add SFX/BGM tags unless requested.
10. **Narrative voice and dialogue.** Let the narrator summarize, guide psychology, connect causality, and shift distance. Keep direct dialogue only when the exact words must be heard, and shape each retained speaker turn so it is clear, breathable, and natural for TTS.

## Segments And Length

- Every segment must contribute meaningful value, but it need not contain all of: obstructed goal, choice, changed information/power/relationship, and immediate consequence. Across the sequence, causality and emotional continuity must still progress.
- Every episode must have its own emotional arc in addition to any cliffhanger.
- If the user does not specify length, ask briefly whether it is one episode or multiple episodes and approximate duration. Compact serial episodes often run around 1,200-2,500 Vietnamese words, roughly 5-15 minutes at 130-160 Vietnamese words/minute. Judge by the ear, not by the page.

## Peak Design

Guardrails prevent bad prose; they do not create a story worth retelling. Every story also needs one designed **peak** — the scene a listener would tell a friend about the next day.

- **Retell test (a quality gate, not a quota):** before drafting, answer in one sentence which planned scene the listener would retell. If none qualifies, the outline is not ready; redesign the collision instead of hoping prose will compensate later.
- **A peak is convergence, not volume.** It is where the most already-planted threads, lies, debts, and characters collide in one place at the highest cost — strongest with witnesses present and no way to retreat. Build it by steering existing threads into the same room, never from a fresh coincidence or a merely louder event.
- **Give the peak disproportionate room.** Stage it at the closest narrative distance with the most concrete detail, at roughly two to three times the space of an ordinary scene. Do not cut away after the first impact; let consequences land on-screen while the situation is still hot.
- **Amplitude is licensed at the peak.** Restraint everywhere else exists to buy range here. Big emotion, big absurdity, or big cost at the peak is not `sến` and not excess — provided setup already paid for it. The sentences stay firm; amplitude comes from situation and cost, not bigger adjectives.
- **One peak per story.** Two "biggest" scenes flatten each other; secondary spikes stay clearly below the main one, and after the peak the story must land in a genuinely changed state, not a reset.

Each genre skill names its usual peak (confrontation, terror set-piece, reveal, relationship choice, comic collision). The peak still obeys causality, fairness, safety, and one-listen clarity; it is simply the one place where scale is mandatory rather than optional.

## Ending Design

Fully answer the Central Question at minimum. A final twist must pay off prior foreshadowing, not introduce a brand-new cheat. Justice/causality may be morally uneven, but the price must match what characters did and lost. A callback ending is optional, not required. If a phrase, object, or habit returns at the end, its changed meaning must be enacted by the situation; never gloss it aloud (`câu đó có nghĩa là...`, `hóa ra`, `không còn là... mà là...`) — a callback the narrator must explain is a callback that failed. The final sentence should leave resonance, not a moral lesson, and a plain concrete sentence often outperforms a polished symmetrical one. A sequel seed is allowed only if it opens a new loop; do not leave the old debt unpaid.

Distinguish debts from bows. Debts — the central question, planted promises, consequential threads — must be paid. Bows are optional: not every minor thread needs symmetrical closure, not every secondary character needs a paired fate, and a small honest remainder (an unexplained detail, a relationship still finding its shape) makes the world feel larger than the plot. Endings where every thread ties off in matching ribbons read as manufactured.

## Fatal Engagement Problems

Do not use: character-profile openings; info-dumps; ambiguous pronouns; pronouns or pointers with no named antecedent; connectors that depend on a premise the story never stated; unstable labels for the same interface/mechanism; premise-critical actions whose method is omitted; passive protagonists; events with no causality; unsetup twists; hiding information the narrator would obviously know; one-note cliffhangers; flat rhythm; unreadable sentences; TTS-hostile punctuation or raw tokens that cause misread timing; unpaid endings; repetitive inner conclusions; dialogue explaining what both people know; long exchanges where pressure/understanding/relationship never changes; multiple speakers packed into one paragraph; narration followed by dialogue repeating the same idea; dialogue written for quoteability; aphorism tennis; forced occupational metaphors; instant psychological diagnosis without evidence; stock domestic/object choreography copied from skill examples; object/system verbs applied to human relationships without an exact character-owned mapping; visible checklist/beat machinery; flat event-report narration; one-note emotional tone; sentimental/flowery cliche (`sến`); description only for beauty; feeling pain on behalf of the listener; wrong Vietnamese address forms; naming systems that mismatch setting; mechanical label rotation; three or more consecutive sentences opening identically without escalation; hindsight interjections (`tôi đâu biết`, `mãi về sau tôi mới hiểu`, `đó là sai lầm đầu tiên`) repeated as episode rhythm; allusive-posturing foreshadows that hint at future irony through an abstract riddle (`đúng cái thứ mình sắp thua`) instead of a concretely named stake in the character's voice; over-abstracted sentences anywhere in the narration where concept nouns or a pseudo-philosophical shape replace the concrete event (`tôi mới biết tên của một văn bản không nói cho tôi biết mình đã nhận món nợ nào`) so the listener must decode who did what with what consequence; most scenes or paragraphs closing on an aphorism, thesis, or punchy fragment triple; restating a fresh reveal in several escalating paraphrases; narrating the new meaning of a callback or symbol instead of enacting it.

## Safety And Sensitive Content

There is no reliable "unsafe word -> safe synonym" table. Judge the whole scene by context, focus, tone, realism, graphic detail, and imitability. Keep events clear but move weight toward choice and consequence. Remove methods, instructions, romanticization, and shock-only details. Review title, thumbnail, captions, and audio as well. Use extra caution for self-harm, sexual violence, and minors. Read [references/an-toan-tu-vung.md](references/an-toan-tu-vung.md) for risk levels, the 7-step editing process, before/after examples, and current policy sources.
