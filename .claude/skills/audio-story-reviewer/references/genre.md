# Genre Contract and Target Alignment

Use this reference when the review must judge whether the story fulfills its promised experience, title/opening promise, audience fit, tone, and genre payoff.

## Genre Contract

A genre contract is the listener's expectation about what kind of pleasure, pressure, and payoff the story promises.

Identify:

- title promise;
- opening promise;
- cover/platform expectation if supplied;
- protagonist desire;
- central dramatic question;
- primary emotional reward;
- expected ending shape: closure, hook, twist, healing, reckoning, dread, laugh, romance payoff.

Do not enforce one universal plot formula. Judge the story by the contract it creates.

## Romance and Love Stories

Check:

- relationship progression is visible;
- attraction, trust, conflict, and vulnerability evolve;
- obstacles are credible and not only external delay;
- both sides change or reveal something meaningful;
- intimacy has specific behavior, not only labels;
- breakup/reunion has cause and cost;
- ending satisfies the chosen tone: HE, SE, BE, open, bittersweet.

Common failures:

- romance resolves without relationship development;
- one side is forgiven without accountability;
- love is declared but never demonstrated through costly action;
- conflict relies on avoidable miscommunication with no character reason;
- rival or family obstacle replaces the central relationship work.

## Family Melodrama

Check:

- wounds are relational, not just event-based;
- duty, shame, hierarchy, money, inheritance, sacrifice, reputation, and filial pressure matter;
- reversals expose hidden loyalties or betrayals;
- reckoning is earned;
- xung ho shifts reflect relationship changes;
- punishment or forgiveness carries consequence.

Common failures:

- villains are cruel without motive or benefit;
- protagonist suffers passively until evidence appears;
- public confrontation resolves too easily;
- family roles are named but not emotionally embodied;
- apology erases harm without residue.

## Revenge

Check:

- injury is specific and morally legible;
- target, method, risk, and cost escalate;
- protagonist makes strategic choices;
- revenge changes the avenger;
- confrontation addresses the original wound;
- victory has moral, relational, or practical consequence.

Common failures:

- revenge plan succeeds by coincidence;
- antagonist collapses without counterplay;
- the injury is vague but punishment is extreme;
- payoff is summarized instead of experienced;
- revenge has no cost, so catharsis feels cheap.

## Mystery and Investigation

Check:

- clues are fair but not obvious;
- suspects or alternatives exist;
- concealment is controlled;
- protagonist uses observation, inference, pressure, or risk;
- reveal answers planted questions;
- hidden information was unavailable for a reason;
- emotional truth and factual truth both matter.

Common failures:

- solution depends on late evidence;
- narrator hides POV-accessible information unfairly;
- clue proves less than the story claims;
- suspect behavior exists only to mislead;
- explanation replaces the discovery scene.

## Horror

Check:

- vulnerability is clear;
- uncertainty escalates;
- rules, if any, are discoverable;
- violation becomes more personal or irreversible;
- explanation does not neutralize dread too early;
- atmosphere serves threat, not decoration;
- consequence persists.

Common failures:

- monster/rule explained into harmlessness;
- fear words replace unsettling events;
- characters ignore obvious survival logic without reason;
- scare repeats without changed cost;
- ending removes dread with a tidy explanation.

## Comedy

Check:

- setup and reversal are clear by ear;
- timing survives audio delivery;
- humor comes from character, situation, status, language, or expectation;
- jokes change pressure or reveal personality;
- running gags escalate or mutate;
- emotional stakes are not destroyed unless satire intends it.

Common failures:

- joke explains itself;
- all characters have the same comic voice;
- punchline depends on visual layout;
- gag repeats without escalation;
- tonal joke lands during an unresolved grief beat and breaks the intended emotion.

## Fantasy, System, Transmigration, Rebirth, Cultivation

Check:

- rules are clear enough for stakes;
- power has cost, limit, hierarchy, or consequence;
- protagonist cannot use special knowledge as an all-purpose answer;
- worldbuilding serves the human problem;
- progression feels earned;
- terms are pronounceable or explained by context for audio.

Common failures:

- rule changes when convenient;
- future knowledge solves everything;
- new power appears at climax;
- lore replaces conflict;
- listeners receive too many names, ranks, and terms at once.

## Healing and Slice of Life

No large external climax is required. Check:

- specificity of routine and place;
- accumulated change;
- emotional honesty;
- small choices with meaning;
- sensory anchors;
- quiet scenes that alter understanding;
- ending with a felt shift.

Common failures:

- nothing changes;
- reflection stays generic;
- routine is described but not transformed;
- conflict is avoided rather than gently resolved;
- ending states healing without showing altered behavior.

## Target Audience and Platform Fit

Consider:

- expected story length;
- episode cliffhanger style;
- tolerance for melodrama, violence, explicit content, or slow burn;
- vocabulary difficulty;
- narrator density;
- age and cultural assumptions;
- whether the piece is for TTS, human narration, short video audio, podcast, or audiobook.

Flag mismatch only when it damages the intended experience.

Examples:

- A dense cast list may work in text but overload a short-form audio opening.
- A highly literary narrator may be beautiful but wrong for a fast revenge platform story.
- A comic aside may suit a humorous family story but break a horror scene's vulnerability.

## Genre Drift

Genre blending is allowed. Drift is a problem when the story promises one experience and forgets to deliver or reframe it.

Signs:

- title promises revenge but story becomes passive suffering;
- opening promises mystery but reveal depends on hidden information;
- romance setup disappears until final confession;
- horror becomes procedural explanation too early;
- comedy tone vanishes without emotional justification;
- family reckoning turns into legal proof only, with no relationship payoff.

Report the drift as:

- promised experience;
- actual delivered experience;
- where the drift begins;
- whether to restore the original promise or reframe the title/opening.
