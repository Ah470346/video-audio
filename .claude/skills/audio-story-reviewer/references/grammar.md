# Grammar, Wording, and Oral Flow

Use only when line-level language is central to a review.

## Check

- spelling, missing/duplicated words, malformed idioms;
- subject-predicate and clause attachment;
- natural Vietnamese collocation and register;
- translated metaphors or object/system language applied unnaturally to people;
- ambiguous pronouns and missing actors;
- punctuation, quotation marks, questions, and speaker turns;
- overloaded sentences or long delayed subjects;
- under-resolved sentence endings;
- repeated low-information fragments;
- repeated syntax, scene-closing theses, reveal restatement, and generic soft-cliché density;
- important raw tokens likely to be misheard.

Do not enforce a word limit. A long sentence with a clear spine may be easier than a short sentence with several referents.

## AI-like Symptoms, Not Proof of AI

Flag only when repeated and harmful:
- the same balanced sentence shell across unrelated scenes;
- all characters speaking in polished abstract language;
- every paragraph ending with insight or punch line;
- repeated hindsight teasers;
- stock gestures/objects replacing scene-specific reaction;
- every action followed by an evaluative clause;
- uniform paragraph and sentence length;
- dialogue built as setup plus perfect comeback.

## Repair Order

1. meaning and grammar;
2. actor/referent/speaker clarity;
3. natural collocation and relationship register;
4. breath and sentence shape;
5. remove repetition/explanation;
6. rhythm and diction polish.

Use evidence, listener impact, and the smallest repair. Do not replace one formula with another.
