# Story Prose Diagnosis And Repair

This is a diagnosis bank for issues summarized in `SKILL.md`. Load only the relevant section. Every quoted object, gesture, occupation, image, name, sentence shape, and repair below is contaminated source material: never reuse or lightly disguise it in a manuscript. Extract the diagnostic principle, then generate any needed repair from the actual scene's place, task, history, evidence, and consequence. A repair may be deletion or plain direct language; it does not need a replacement device.

## 1. Sentence-Level Stiffness

### Symptom: Pronoun Has No Clear Antecedent

**Stiff:**

> Đứa bé vừa đạp một cú làm tôi nghẹn thở, còn người đàn ông sắp làm chồng tôi tin một tờ giấy hơn tin chính nó.

`Nó` may mean the baby, the paper, or an abstract idea. "Believing the baby itself" is also unnatural here.

**Repair:**

> Đứa bé trong bụng khẽ đạp. Tôi đặt tay lên bụng, nhìn người sắp làm chồng mình chọn tin tờ xét nghiệm giả trước mặt hai trăm khách mời.

Keep one body action, name the object being believed, and remove the forced comparison.

### Symptom: One Sentence Carries Too Many Information Layers

**Stiff:**

> Tôi đứng giữa sảnh tiệc, nhớ đến cuốn sách đã đọc trước khi tỉnh dậy trong thân xác này, nơi nhân vật vốn sẽ mất tất cả sau khi bị em gái hãm hại, nên tôi biết mình phải bình tĩnh dù đứa bé vừa đạp và mọi người đang chờ tôi khóc.

**Repair:**

> Cả sảnh tiệc đang chờ tôi khóc.
>
> Tôi đặt tay lên bụng. Cảnh này tôi đã đọc rồi. Trong truyện, Tô Vy Anh bước ra khỏi cánh cửa kia và không bao giờ trở lại.

Separate present, memory, and consequence into the order of awareness.

### Symptom: Sentence Explains Itself

**Stiff:**

> Tôi siết tờ giấy trong tay vì tức giận trước sự phản bội của anh.

**Repair:**

> Mép giấy cứa vào lòng bàn tay. Tôi vẫn không buông.

If the situation already lets listeners infer anger and betrayal, do not name both.

## 2. Scene-Level Disconnection

Signs:

- Next scene opens with new information not caused by the previous scene.
- A side character appears mainly to deliver needed secret.
- Major action lacks reaction/decision bridge.
- Summary uses only "then/after that/at the same time."

Repair with "therefore / but":

**Disconnected:**

> Cô giúp việc tìm thấy hóa đơn. Sau đó tôi đến phòng khám. Ở đó tôi lấy được bằng chứng.

**Connected:**

> Tôi lục lại đôi giày em gái đã mượn và thấy một mảnh tem gửi đồ mắc dưới đế. Mã người nhận dẫn tới phòng khám. Nhưng quầy tiếp tân từ chối cung cấp hồ sơ, nên tôi buộc phải dùng chính lịch khám của mình để đặt một cái bẫy.

The clue comes from protagonist action; the obstacle forces a tactic change; the next scene follows from the previous.

## 3. Thin Plotting

### Motivation Summarized Instead Of Proven

**Thin:**

> Mẹ tôi làm tất cả vì luôn thương em gái hơn tôi và muốn chiếm công ty.

**Repair through evidence chain:**

- She corrects "hai đứa" into "em con" while dividing assets.
- She stays silent when fake documents appear but knows exactly which page to open.
- When cornered, she does not apologize; she asks whether the protagonist will break the father's contract.

Three behaviors prove favoritism, knowledge, and goal. No need for a full psychology lecture.

### Climax Wins By Paperwork

Documents are tools. Ask:

- What sample/person/object created it?
- Who identified it?
- Who held it?
- How long did it take to obtain?
- What exact claim does it prove?

A consultation invoice does not prove forgery by itself. A DNA result without an identified reference sample does not prove paternity by itself. A climax needs opponent rebuttal, public pressure, real cost, or a choice between two interests. Evidence must be used through a risky tactic.

### Protagonist Knows But Does Nothing

If they know an event is coming, make them try to prevent it with a reasonable plan. The plan can fail because of missing data, opponent adaptation, or the prevention attempt causing a new problem. Do not let them sit and wait because the author needs the original scene to happen.

## 4. Flat Or Overdone Emotion

### Flat

Problem: heavy event happens, character instantly delivers a sharp line and moves to perfect plan.

Repair: give enough time or prose space for receiving the event, self-control, and decision. This may be a direct statement, changed conduct, a failed attempt, silence, or a later consequence. Do not automatically insert repeated reading, a facedown phone, a held object, or a tidy delayed answer.

### Overdone

Problem: heartbeat, shaking, tears, spinning head, collapsing world all at once.

Repair with one response that the exact situation requires, or name the emotion directly. A domestic object or small hand movement is not personal merely because it is concrete.

### Narration And Description Duplicate

**Too much:**

> “Tôi ổn,” tôi nói, nhưng tay run lên vì tôi thực sự rất sợ.

**Repair:** keep the spoken denial and remove the explanatory gloss when context already carries fear. Add physical evidence only when it changes blocking, consequence, or interpretation; do not add a generic cup, spoon, hand, breath, or gaze beat.

## 5. Awkward Dialogue

### Dialogue Reports To The Listener

**Awkward:**

> “Chị còn nhớ mười năm trước mẹ đã đưa em sang nước ngoài vì thầy bói nói hai chúng ta khắc nhau không?”

Both sisters know this; the line serves the author.

**Repair direction:** let one speaker ask only what they would need to know now; place the withheld history in a later consequence, a relationship-specific refusal, or narration whose timing has been earned. Do not smuggle backstory through a stock object reveal or clothing gesture.

### Everyone Sounds The Same

Create speech fingerprints for recurring characters:

| Element | Question |
|---|---|
| Length | brief or explanatory? |
| Vocabulary | everyday, professional, academic, old-fashioned? |
| Tactic | counters with questions, silence, sarcasm, soothing? |
| Forbidden words | what do they never say directly? |
| Losing control | louder, overly polite, bare-name address? |

Voice differences come from worldview and goal, not only catchphrases.

### Too Realistic Dialogue

Fictional natural speech is not a transcript. Cut filler, greetings, and loops that do not work. Keep hesitation or tangent only when it reveals avoidance, power, or emotion.

### Forced Occupational Metaphor And Aphorism Tennis

Problem: the author turns a character's profession into a governing image field, then makes another speaker borrow that field to cue a perfect reply.

**Stiff:**

> “Tám năm của chúng ta hỏng ở đâu?”
>
> “Anh sửa được đồ vật, sao không sửa được hôn nhân?”
>
> “Đồ vật không giả vờ rằng nó vẫn ổn.”

The exchange is clear and concrete, but that does not make it natural. `Hỏng/sửa/đồ vật` reduces an interpersonal history to a repair category, and the second speaker exists momentarily to set up the third line. Both characters sound like one author writing a caption.

**Repair direction:**

1. State each speaker's immediate human intention in plain Vietnamese.
2. Name the behavior, need, or avoidance the object language was hiding.
3. Give the speakers separate vocabularies and timing.
4. Preserve a marked metaphor only if the speaker owns it and ordinary paraphrase loses real action or subtext.

Rewrite in each character's ordinary diction, with no obligation to preserve a comeback or make the exchange quotable. Let uncertainty, evasion, or incomplete understanding remain when that is truthful.

Do not repair this class by changing `hỏng` to `nứt`, `vỡ`, or another object word. Run the full [human semantic-fit gate](../../audio-story-engagement/references/ngon-ngu-con-nguoi.md); the defect is category fit, speaker ownership, and over-finished dialogue, not one banned token.

## 6. Address Forms At Wrong Relationship Temperature

Map by speaking/listening pair, not one fixed pronoun per character:

| Speaker -> listener | Normal | Public | When cracked | Reason for shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| wife -> husband | em/anh | em/anh | tôi/anh or bare name | create distance |
| child -> mother | con/mẹ | con/mẹ | con/bà only at extreme rupture | break family role |
| employee -> boss | tôi/anh, em/anh by age/culture | tôi/giám đốc | tôi/ông | formal confrontation |

This is an inference example, not a mechanical table. Context, era, region, and world rules may change choices.

Common errors:

1. **Self/address mismatch:** one line `em/anh`, next `tôi/cậu` without changed relationship.
2. **Wrong shade:** using `nó` for a fetus in a protective mother's POV makes emotion colder; using `cô ấy` between intimate spouses creates unintended distance.
3. **Unbridged shift:** Vietnamese address shifts are strong; place them after an action/reveal that changes relationship temperature.

## 7. AI-Prose Traces

Do not ban individual structures. Diagnose when they repeat and replace specificity.

| Trace | Why stiff | Repair direction |
|---|---|---|
| `Tôi không khóc. Không phải vì... Mà vì...` | repeated declaration rhythm | use action and specific information |
| `Tôi biết X. Nhưng tôi không biết Y.` | visible hook formula | let character use X and collide with Y |
| `Điều tôi không ngờ là...` | tells the turn before it happens | present the sound/object/line directly |
| `Mọi thứ chỉ mới bắt đầu.` | empty hook usable anywhere | end on specific activated danger |
| three same-syntax sentences | speech rhythm, not living thought | vary length/function; cut weakest line |
| paragraph ends with philosophy | narrator lectures instead of living | keep only if voice-specific and challenged by action |
| profession becomes the metaphor for every human issue | occupation colonizes character and scene language | express competence through attention/action first; keep only owned, exact mappings |
| metaphor followed by perfect counter-metaphor | speakers co-author a quote instead of pursuing separate goals | return both turns to plain intention; let the reply answer behavior rather than image |
| abstract "làm dáng sâu sắc" reflection: vague referent / paradox / negation-as-depth / mind-ledger / `nạn nhân của...` | a **class**, not one phrase — reaches for depth via abstraction instead of a concrete image | run the one-listen test; name the referent, keep the image/action, cut the gloss |

### Instant Psychological Omniscience

**Symptom:** a character with little contact turns one cue into certainty about another person's hidden habit, wound, motive, or lifelong defense; the narration then confirms the diagnosis as truth.

Audit four levels separately:

1. **Observation:** what was actually said or done?
2. **Inference:** what could the observer reasonably suspect now?
3. **Pattern:** what would require repeated contact across situations?
4. **Truth:** what would require confession, evidence, or retrospective knowledge?

Repair by lowering certainty, asking a question, allowing a wrong guess, planting prior contact, or moving the conclusion later. A clever line is not evidence. Professional expertise may support a narrower observation; it does not grant access to a stranger's private history.

### Skill-Example Contamination And Stock Object Choreography

**Symptom:** unrelated scenes repeatedly use domestic props, handheld objects, tidying motions, reading something multiple times, delayed replies, lowered gazes, or perfectly timed small gestures to manufacture interiority. The detail may be concrete yet interchangeable.

Run two tests:

- **Object-presence:** if the object disappears, do blocking, meaning, causality, or consequence change?
- **Scene-origin:** can the detail be derived from this place, current task, relationship history, and immediate pressure without reference to any craft example?

If both answers are no, delete the choreography. Do not replace one stock object with another. Direct naming, a practical response, an omitted beat, or a later consequence may be stronger.

### Visible Checklist Prose

**Symptom:** every scene exposes the same craft sequence—goal statement, obstacle, symbolic gesture, realization, quotable closer—or every dialogue turn behaves like a named tactic. The text is orderly but feels manufactured.

Repair at the sequence level. Keep the actual event and human pressure; remove compulsory miniature turns, thesis endings, matching metaphors, and repeated realization phrases. Allow some scenes to absorb, accumulate, orient, miss, misunderstand, or carry residue without announcing what changed.

### False-Profundity Abstract Closer (A Class, Not One Phrase)

This is **a class of sentence, not a fixed phrase and not only the final line.** The defect is any reflective sentence that reaches for depth through abstraction, paradox, or negation instead of a concrete person, object, or action, so it lands as `khó hiểu và rời rạc` on one hearing even though every word is grammatical. It appears most often as a scene or paragraph closer, but the same failure can sit mid-paragraph. Do not match it by memorizing the shells below — they are examples of the family. Match it with the test.

**The one-listen test — run it on every reflective or closing sentence.** Read the sentence aloud once and ask:

1. **Concrete referent?** Does it name a real person, object, or action, or does it float on abstractions — `một người`, `một điều gì đó`, `một thứ`, `một khoảng`, `cách duy nhất`, `con số của chính mình`, `một ngăn không tên`?
2. **Parses on first hearing?** Or does a paradox (`giữ một người đã không còn ở lại`) or a stack of negations force the listener to re-run it?
3. **Adds or restates?** Does it add a new image or fact, or only re-explain what the concrete lines above already showed?
4. **Ten-story test:** could this exact sentence be pasted into ten other stories?

Fail two or more, and it is the defect. Repair by naming the concrete referent, keeping the image or action, and deleting the gloss — never by swapping one abstraction for a smoother one. If nothing concrete remains, end the paragraph one sentence earlier.

**Common shapes in this family (non-exhaustive).** Each `→` shows the direction of repair, not a fixed rewrite:

- **vague referent hiding a named one:** `giữ một người đã không còn ở lại` → name it: `giữ được mẹ, dù mẹ không còn ở đây`;
- **paradox stated for effect:** `yêu một người mình chưa từng gặp` → show it: `đêm nào cũng đọc lại lá thư của người chưa từng gặp mặt`;
- **negation-as-depth** (the payload is a `chưa từng` / `không bao giờ` / `không tên`): `thứ tôi chưa bao giờ gọi được tên` → name the feeling plainly, or cut the sentence;
- **singularity gloss** (`duy nhất`, `cuối cùng`, `chưa bao giờ ... đến thế`): `đó là cách duy nhất tôi biết để tồn tại` → name the actual behavior/constraint or cut the manifesto; do not insert an arbitrary action;
- **mind-ledger / self-referential meta:** `tôi ghi vào một ngăn không tên trong lòng`, `xếp vào sổ trong đầu` → keep the image, drop the invented filing cabinet;
- **abstract predication:** `tôi là nạn nhân của đúng một ánh mắt` → `một ánh mắt nhìn lâu hơn thường lệ, vậy mà tôi nhớ mãi`;
- **allusive-posturing foreshadow** (a hint at future irony wrapped in a riddle): `tôi đâu biết mình vừa đặt cược bằng đúng cái thứ mình sắp thua` → keep the foreshadow but buy it concretely, in the narrator's own texture: `tôi tự tin tới mức đã bắt đầu nghĩ xem nên gọi vị trà sữa nào trước` (the state shown as a comic action) or `tôi không hề biết một tháng trà sữa của mình đã bắt đầu đếm ngược` (hindsight kept, stake named); delete only when no concrete version earns its place;
- **abstract summary bolted onto a concrete list** → cut the summary and let the concrete list stand.

Apply the shape test to the manuscript's own line. Name the actual referent or behavior already established, or cut the reflective gloss. Do not invent tea, kitchens, ledgers, routines, a gaze, or another concrete image just to make abstraction look literary. The correct repair may be to end the paragraph one sentence earlier.

### Abstract Evaluation Without Concrete Meaning

This defect is related to false profundity but broader. It can appear anywhere, including ordinary mid-scene narration, when the narrator uses an evaluative label whose private criterion never reaches the listener. The sentence may be grammatically correct and contain no metaphor at all, yet remain hard to understand.

Run the **target -> criterion -> evidence** test:

1. **Target:** what exact word, action, person, object, or relationship is being judged?
2. **Criterion:** what does `an toàn`, `có đời sống`, `chân thật`, `có trọng lượng`, `có chiều sâu`, `đủ thật`, or `có ý nghĩa` mean in this scene?
3. **Evidence/consequence:** what audible detail, behavior, contrast, or result proves that judgment?

If two answers are missing, the sentence asks the listener to invent the narrator's editorial vocabulary. Rewrite it as the concrete defect or consequence. Do not repair it by substituting another abstract adjective.

**Hard to understand (criteria omitted):**

> Câu ấy đúng ngữ pháp, an toàn và không có chút đời sống nào.

`An toàn` does not say safe from what. `Không có đời sống` does not say which human history or conflict is absent. The sentence announces a verdict without giving the listener its basis.

**Clear repair:**

> Câu đó không sai, nhưng nghe như một bản thông cáo. Nó không cho khán giả biết hai người đã sống với nhau ra sao hay vì sao phải ly hôn.

**Compact repair when the shared history is already established:**

> Câu đó không sai, nhưng giống lời quảng cáo hơn lời của hai người từng sống với nhau tám năm.

Other shapes in this class:

- `Lời anh ta có trọng lượng.` -> show who stops, obeys, hesitates, or changes course, and why his words can cause that response.
- `Cảnh ấy đủ thật.` -> name the ordinary detail or imperfect reaction that makes it recognizable.
- `Mối quan hệ của họ có chiều sâu.` -> show the shared habit, old debt, private shorthand, or costly choice that belongs to this pair.
- `Đó là một khoảnh khắc có ý nghĩa.` -> state what decision, relationship, or consequence changed at that moment.

An abstract term is not banned. It is allowed when the surrounding sentence or scene has already made its criterion concrete. The rule is not “never say `an toàn`”; it is “never make the listener guess what your evaluation means here.”

### Ten-Story Test

Take a suspicious line and replace names. If it still fits ten other dramas, it is too generic. Repair from the scene's specific cause, choice, consequence, relationship, or voice. Do not assume adding an object makes it specific.

## 8. Hard Case: Foreknowledge Premise

Foreknowledge easily becomes stiff because the narrator must explain two reality layers and can become omnipotent.

Check four boundaries:

1. **Source:** book, reincarnation, system, scattered memory?
2. **Resolution:** remembers outcome only, or cause, time, exact lines?
3. **Reliability:** is book absolutely true; can memory distort?
4. **Change model and opposition:** how does new action alter future/plot; what proof, trust, execution, or ethical limits remain?

Knowledge does not need to cost lifespan/points every use. It only needs to not automatically solve the main genre reward.

Avoid vague meta narration:

**Stiff:**

> Tôi đã đọc cảnh này, viết sẵn từng chữ, trong cuốn tiểu thuyết tôi gấp lại trước khi mở mắt trong thân xác nhân vật sắp chết vì nó.

`Nó` is unclear; "character" and "novel" pull listeners out of the wedding.

**Repair:**

> Cảnh này tôi đã đọc rồi.
>
> Trong truyện, Tô Vy Anh rời khỏi sảnh tiệc, sinh non ba tuần sau đó rồi biến mất khỏi câu chuyện bằng đúng một dòng.

Say it once, name the consequence, and return to the present. Prove foreknowledge through character choice, not repeated "in the book/script/game board" references.

Good model:

> Knowing **the result** -> plan based on assumed **cause** -> discover different cause -> choose between clinging to original plot and saving what exists now.

Foreknowledge creates pressure and error, not the removal of surprise.

## 9. VoxCPM/TTS Pause And Dialogue Readiness

Use this section for surface repairs that affect how the story is heard after TTS rendering. For the full operating rules, read `../../audio-story-engagement/references/voxcpm-tts-ngat-nghi.md`.

### Symptom: Dialogue Looks Fast And Collapsed

**Risky:**

> "Ra ngoài." "Không." "Tôi bảo cô ra ngoài."

The page shows separate quotes, but a single-voice TTS pass can read them as one fast stream.

**Repair:**

> "Ra ngoài."
>
> "Không."
>
> Anh hạ giọng. "Tôi bảo cô ra ngoài."

Separate speaker turns, add one meaningful action beat, and let the paragraph break create the reset.

### Symptom: One Quoted Line Carries Too Many Moves

**Risky:**

> "Khoan em chưa nói xong anh đừng ký tờ đó vì nếu anh ký thì mẹ anh sẽ dùng nó để khóa tài khoản của em và anh sẽ không bao giờ biết ai đổi mẫu xét nghiệm."

The line contains warning, command, cause, consequence, and reveal in one breath.

**Repair:**

> "Khoan. Em chưa nói xong."
>
> Tôi đặt tay lên mép tờ giấy. "Anh ký vào đó, mẹ anh sẽ khóa tài khoản của em."
>
> Anh nhìn xuống dòng chữ cuối cùng. Tôi nói tiếp: "Và anh sẽ không bao giờ biết ai đã đổi mẫu xét nghiệm."

Break by tactical move: stop -> immediate danger -> evidence/reveal.

### Symptom: Visual Tokens Break Spoken Rhythm

**Risky:**

> Tin nhắn gửi lúc 19:45, kèm mã hồ sơ ADN số 03/2024.

**Repair:**

> Tin nhắn gửi lúc bảy giờ bốn mươi lăm tối, kèm mã hồ sơ xét nghiệm ADN đầu tháng tư năm đó.

Write the intended spoken form unless the exact code is story-critical. If the exact code matters, record a production note outside the pure story file.

### Symptom: Decorative Pause Marks Replace Meaning

**Risky:**

> "Em..." cô nói, "em chỉ muốn..."

This can be correct when hesitation is the point, but repeated ellipses quickly become a verbal tic and can make TTS drag.

**Repair direction:**

- Keep the ellipsis only where the thought trails off.
- Use a period for a deliberate stop.
- Use action or silence when the pause itself reveals fear, calculation, or refusal.

If the pause matters, give it a sentence boundary or leave an audible silence through paragraphing. Add an action only when it changes who can act, what is physically happening, or how the next line is understood; never insert a key-on-table beat as generic resolve.

### Symptom: Consecutive Sentences Open With The Same Words

The audit flags this as `mở-đầu-lặp-liên-tiếp`. It is the one defect class in this section that **cannot** be found by listening to the audio for artifacts, because the audio is correct. It has been mistaken for a render bug in production: a listener reported a chunk as stuttering, and re-measuring the waveform proved the synthesis was accurate to the script. Re-rendering can never fix it.

Why the page and the ear disagree: a renderer splits prose into short chunks, and each chunk begins after a prosodic reset. Two chunks that open with the same words at the same metrical position sound like the synthesizer repeating itself, no matter how deliberate the repetition looked in the manuscript.

**First decide whether the repetition is doing work.**

- **It is doing work** when the repeated opening builds toward a payoff — an anaphora that lands on a turn, a refusal, or a reveal. Keep the device and repair the rhythm.
- **It is accidental** when consecutive sentences merely happen to start alike. Rewrite the openings outright; there is nothing to preserve.

**Repair for deliberate anaphora — keep the anchor, change the sentence-initial word.**

1. Identify the anchor: the words that actually carry the device. Those stay in every sentence.
2. Make the **first word** of each sentence different: drop the subject in one instance, front an adverb or connective in another, invert the clause. The chunk reset stresses the first word; that is the only position where variation is guaranteed to be heard.
3. Do **not** repair by inserting a short unstressed word after the same first word (`Tôi muốn nói` → `Tôi rất muốn nói` → `Tôi còn muốn nói`). This was tried in production and failed twice: both renders measured word-perfect (CTC likelihood ratio > 25 nats for the scripted word, Whisper CER 0.0), and the listener still reported "tôi tôi muốn nói". The inserted function word is unstressed, ~80 ms at narration speed, wedged between a chunk-initial stressed word and the anchor — a primed ear swallows it. The audit's fuzzy rule now flags this shape (`mở-đầu-lặp-liên-tiếp`): same first word plus three of the first four words matching.
4. Prefer variation that also escalates meaning, so the ear reads build rather than noise (`Tôi muốn nói` → `Muốn nói cả` → `Càng muốn nói`).
5. Check the sound, not only the spelling: the new sentence-initial words should differ in onset consonant and vowel, not just in tone mark.
6. Check the sentence bodies. If they are also the same length, that compounds the problem; let them grow, so the passage escalates in duration as well as in wording.

**Repair for accidental repetition:** change the syntactic frame of one sentence rather than hunting for a synonym. Move a clause to the front, turn one sentence into a question or a fragment, or merge two sentences so the second opening disappears.

Do not fix this by deleting the repetition wholesale. Anaphora is one of the strongest devices available in narrated audio; the defect is uniform rhythm, not repetition itself. A labeled-list shape (`Ngày thứ nhất: ...`) is also a valid keep: the label carries stress and the colon carries a pause, so the ear tracks it as a list; the audit exempts it.

**Sentence-level verification is sufficient.** The chunker never reorders sentences, so any two sentences heard back to back are exactly two adjacent sentences in the manuscript, whatever chunk each lands in. Re-run the audit after the repair; it now checks both exact and near-identical (one-word-insert) openings.
